At the corridor exit $D$, the true futures of exiting and failing to exit differ sharply: exiting spreads probability mass across the room while failing leaves the agent stuck at the exit cell. FB's fit assigns both actions a nearly identical, diffuse future, so its critic barely prefers the precise exit action over a failed one, and this margin collapses as the failed exit becomes more persistent (larger $k$). Density-FB keeps the two futures distinct and keeps the margin close to the truth at every $k$ in closed form (lines: FB is the deterministic rank-8 SVD optimum of its squared loss, Density-FB the cross-entropy optimum, mean over 5 seeds with a shaded 95\% CI, t-interval (95\%, scipy, df=4)), and the TD-trained tabular versions of the same two losses (hollow diamonds: 20k steps on 100k random-policy transitions, mean and 95\% CI over 5 seeds) preserve the ordering, though TD-trained FB is seed-noisy (its interval reaches zero at $k{=}1$, 10, 20) and TD-trained Density-FB overshoots the true margin; maps use $k{=}20$, $d{=}8$, seed 0, one color scale per column shared between its two rows.
