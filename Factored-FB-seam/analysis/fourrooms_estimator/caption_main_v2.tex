This figure shows the true, FB, and Density-FB successor-measure rows out of the sticky doorway $D$ at k=20, d=8, $\gamma$=0.98 (door maps use Density seed 0): crossing the door (top row) versus failing to cross (bottom row), each cell shaded by one shared truncated-Blues colormap (light = little occupancy mass, dark navy = concentrated mass, identical for true/FB/Density-FB) under a PowerNorm(gamma=0.6) fixed per row to the true row's own range, so darkness alone carries the comparison; total-variation distance to the true row is printed under each fit (FB's negative entries, min $\approx$ -0.001, are clipped to 0 for display and for that TV number), plus the normalized action gap at $D$ versus the stickiness parameter $k$ at rank $d=8$ (right; true = blue dashed, FB = orange deterministic line, Density = green mean line with a shaded 95\% CI band across 5 independently seeded fits, t-interval (95\%, scipy, df=4)). A gap of 1 means the fit matches the true value difference between crossing and failing to cross; a gap of 0 means the fit cannot tell the two actions apart, and FB's gap falls toward 0 as the doorway gets stickier while Density's stays close to the truth, matching the larger TV distance and paler, more spread-out darkness pattern already visible for FB at k=20 on the left. Both fits see exactly the same successor-measure targets and neither uses TD training or an actor: FB's occupancies are the closed-form rank-$d$ optimum of its squared loss (the truncated SVD of $m/\rho$ in the $\rho$-weighted norm), while Density-FB's are a rank-$d$ softmax model fit by cross-entropy. In the action-value experiment (Q(D,a) over the continuous 2D action plane), FB's argmax stays inside the crossing cone at d=8, $\gamma$=0.98 for every k tested even as its margin collapses (the normalized gap falls to 0.41 by k=40) -- the fit keeps the ranking right while losing the separation. In the discrete 4-action sweep (gap.csv), FB's argmax at $D$ does flip away from $a_{cross}$ for the room reward at: $\gamma$=0.9, d=4, k=10 (norm.\ gap -0.01); $\gamma$=0.9, d=4, k=20 (norm.\ gap -0.06); $\gamma$=0.9, d=4, k=40 (norm.\ gap -0.03).
