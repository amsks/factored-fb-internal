\section{The two agents, and the design decisions they expose}
\label{sec:decisions}
% ======================================================================
Both agents parameterize a goal/task value as the \emph{same} normalized inner
product and steer it with the \emph{same} flow (FQL) actor. They differ only in a
handful of design decisions. GCIQL learns a goal-conditioned optimal value by IQL:
\[
Q(s,a,g)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt d}\phi(s,a)^\top\psi(g),\quad
\mathcal L_V=\mathbb E\big[L_2^\tau(\bar Q{-}V)\big],\ \
\mathcal L_Q=\mathbb E\big[(r{+}\gamma(1{-}d)V'{-}Q)^2\big],\ \
r=\mathbb 1[s{\approx}g]{-}1,
\]
where the expectile $L_2^\tau$ ($\tau{>}\tfrac12$) is an implicit $\max_a$
(policy improvement). FB instead fits the discounted successor measure of a policy
\emph{family} $\{\pi_z\}$ --- \emph{no reward, no max}:
\[
M^{\pi_z}(s,a,\mathrm ds')\approx F(s,a,z)^\top B(s')\rho(\mathrm ds'),
\]
\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FB}}=\underbrace{\mathbb E[(F^\top B'{-}\gamma\,\mathrm{sg}[\bar F^\top\bar B'])^2]}_{\text{TD over random }s'\sim\rho}
-2\,\mathbb E[F^\top B^{+}]+\lambda\|\mathbb E[BB^\top]{-}I\|_F^2,
\]
with a post-hoc linear task readout $z_r=\mathbb E_{s'}[r(s')B(s')]$,
$Q_z=F(s,a,z)^\top z$. The eight decisions that separate them (each a knob in the
JAX GCIQL agent \texttt{gciql\_flow.py}, or the FB torch agent
\texttt{agents/fb/agent.py} for the FB-side rows) are:

\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{3pt}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}c >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{1.9cm} Y Y >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{2.75cm}@{}}
\toprule

\midrule
\B{D1} & Value objective & goal-reward IQL value-TD (expectile $\max$) & successor-measure density (no $\max$) & \texttt{measure\_only} \\
\B{D2} & Critic form & bilinear $\phi^\top\psi/\sqrt d$ (or MLP) & bilinear $F^\top B/\sqrt d$ & \texttt{value\_arch},\newline\texttt{fwd\_two\_tower} \\

\noindent{\footnotesize Knob column shows the JAX GCIQL names
(\texttt{gciql\_flow.py}); the FB torch agent (\texttt{agents/fb/agent.py}) implements
the Direction-B / FB-side rows with parallel knobs under different names --- D1
\texttt{critic\_mode}, D3 \texttt{decondition\_F}, D5 \texttt{d\_norm} --- and D8's
\texttt{train\_goal\_ratio}/\texttt{decouple\_actor\_z} exist \emph{only} there (no
\texttt{gciql\_flow.py} equivalent).}

\noindent Two differences are bundled inside \B{D1}: the \emph{backup signal}
(reward-value vs.\ occupancy density) and the \emph{improvement operator}
(expectile $\max$ vs.\ none). D1 is the lever; D2--D8 are the structure it rides on.

\section{How we built the ladder}
% ======================================================================
Two anchors: \B{IQL-bilinear} $\approx\good{90}$ and \B{FB-measure}
$\approx\bad{34}$ (@500k; $\to48$ @1M, still climbing). We morph \emph{one
decision at a time} in two directions:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \B{Direction A (GCIQL$\to$FB): impose FB structure on the working value.}
  Start at IQL-bilinear ($90$) and switch on D2--D5 one at a time. \emph{Does FB's
  structure survive a value that works?}
\item \B{Direction B (FB$\to$GCIQL): import the value objective into FB.}
  Start at FB-measure ($34$) and swap in D1 (and relax D3--D5). \emph{Does the
  value objective rescue FB's representation?}
\end{itemize}
The flow actor (D7) is held \emph{fixed} across every state row except the explicit
actor isolation (\S\ref{sec:actor}). Table~\ref{tab:master} is every combination we
ran, grouped by direction; \S\ref{sec:isolations} then isolates each decision.

\section{Rank is a floor, not a ceiling}
\label{sec:rank}

A natural reading of the bilinear/MLP gap is that the inner-product head helps by
\emph{compressing} --- a low-rank bottleneck on the value function. The rank ladder refutes
this. Capacity is not being restricted: everything from $d{=}28$ to $d{=}512$ sits in a flat
band, and only $d{=}5$ --- too few dimensions to span the goal space at all --- collapses.

\begin{center}\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}l r r@{}}
\toprule
Critic (IQL-trained $+$ flow) & Succ. @500k & \\
\midrule
bilinear $d5$    & \bad{$13.3\pm4.4$} & rank below the span of the goal set \\
\midrule
bilinear $d28$   & \good{$86.4\pm7.5$} & \\
bilinear $d50$   & \good{$91.6\pm3.1$} & flat band: above the floor, \\
bilinear $d128$  & \good{$93.1\pm0.7$} & rank is free \\
bilinear $d512$  & \good{$87.7\pm1.4$} & \\
\midrule
MLP critic       & $71.3\pm3.4$ & unconstrained in $g$; below \emph{every} bilinear rank \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent At $d{=}512$ the goal leg is as wide as the hidden layers, so the bilinear form is
barely a capacity restriction at all --- and it still beats the MLP by $16$ points. What the
inner product constrains is not \emph{how much} the critic can express but \emph{how the goal
enters}: $Q(s,a,g)=\langle\phi(s,a),\psi(g)\rangle/\sqrt d$ with $\psi$ a function of $g$
alone. \S\ref{sec:ood} shows that this is exactly the axis on which the MLP fails.

\noindent This relocates the failure. The measure objective does not simply make a weaker
agent --- it makes an agent that is \emph{competent on the data manifold and cannot carry that
competence to a new goal}. And it explains the bilinear head: with $\psi$ a function of $g$
alone, $Q(\cdot,\cdot,g)$ for \emph{any} goal lies in the linear span of $Q(\cdot,\cdot,g_i)$
for $d$ dataset goals spanning $\psi$-space, so a novel evaluation goal needs no
extrapolation of the value function --- only of the small map $\psi$. The MLP, nonlinear in
$g$, has no such guarantee, and pays $16$ points for it. Consistently, letting the goal leg
see the state ($\psi(s,g)$, destroying exactly this property) does not merely revert to MLP
quality: it destabilizes the IQL fixed point outright ($32.5\pm39.4$; $2/3$ seeds diverge
late, $q$ crossing positive; the one stable seed lands at $69.2$, i.e.\ MLP level).

$86.4{\to}87.5$ and ortho$_1$ $86.4{\to}85.5$ (within noise); under the FB-IQL
import, $\sqrt d$ helps a little ($58.7{\to}65.3$), de-conditioning $F{\to}\phi$ is
flat ($58.7{\to}58.0$). Structure is second-order; the objective is first-order.

\paragraph{D6 --- task entry / $z$-source.} Routing the actor's task vector from
the critic's $\psi$ (rather than the value net) under the measure/graft objective
scores \bad{22.0}. The clean exact-$z$ vs.\ inferred-$z$ \emph{dissociation}
(G4) lives at 1M and is in \S\ref{sec:1M}.

\paragraph{D7 --- actor.}
\label{sec:actor}
Held fixed across every state row --- a control, not a cause. On the \emph{same}
bilinear value, flow $\approx90$ vs.\ DDPG+BC $\approx55$: the flow actor is needed
to \emph{realize} the bilinear value, but because it is held identical across every
FB-vs-GCIQL comparison it cannot explain the gap.

\paragraph{D8 --- goal/task sampling distribution \& actor--critic decoupling.}
The frankenstein knob easiest to leave implicit, so state it plainly. FB and GCIQL
disagree not only on the \emph{objective} but on \emph{which tasks the value and the
actor ever see}. Canonical FB samples the task $z$ as \emph{random directions} in
latent space and shows the \emph{same} distribution to the forward map, the backward
map, and the actor: one policy family $\{\pi_z\}$ meant to cover all of latent space.
GCIQL never samples a random task --- it relabels goals by hindsight from the data
and \emph{splits} the distribution: the value learns from a \emph{mix}
(trajectory-future goals $+$ the current state, which pins $V{=}0$ at the goal, $+$
some random goals for coverage), while the actor trains \emph{only} on
trajectory-future goals, so it practices only \emph{reachable} behaviour. Grafting
one agent's objective onto the other's form inherits a free choice here, and it is a
real confound: perhaps FB underperforms not because of the measure objective but
because its actor is trained to chase random $z$ it can never reach. Two knobs
isolate it. \B{HER task sampling (zHER)} replaces FB's random-direction $z$ with
trajectory-future relabelled $z$, matching FB's task distribution to GCIQL's goals.
\B{Decouple-actor-$z$} gives the actor GCIQL's trajectory-only distribution while the
critic keeps FB's broad one --- FB's own version of GCIQL's value/actor split. If the
distribution were the lever, either should push FB toward GCIQL. \emph{Neither does:}
zHER $32$, decouple-actor-$z$ $39$ (measure) / $20$ (IQL) / $18$ (zHER critic goals)
--- all \emph{below} plain FB ($48$; all @1M, zHER $n{=}10$ / decouple $n{=}3$). Matching the task distribution to
GCIQL, and splitting actor from critic exactly as GCIQL does, never lifts FB above
its own ceiling. The distribution is second-order; \B{D1 is the lever}. (GCIQL's
value/actor split is itself already default-ON in every Direction-B row above; these
knobs push it \emph{further} toward FB's broad critic distribution and still fail.)

\section{What the ladder says}
\label{sec:whatitsays}
% ======================================================================
By elimination across the isolations: the \B{actor} (flow, held fixed, $73$ on a
good critic), the \B{bilinear form} ($\ge$MLP), \B{rank} ($d50$ suffices), \B{norm}
and \B{$z$-fwd} (flat) are all exonerated on cube. The binding ingredient is
\B{D1 --- the learning objective:} end-to-end IQL value-TD vs.\ measure-matching,
confirmed from both directions (IQL$\to$FB survives to $87$; FB$\to$IQL and
measure-rebuild cap at $16$--$65$). \B{Mechanism (proposed):} a measure objective
shapes the representation to reconstruct the discounted state-occupancy density,
which on manipulation is dominated by where the \emph{arm} goes; the thin,
interaction-conditioned successor structure that decides whether a grasp is
\emph{kept} through transport is a low-mass correction the density has no incentive
to resolve into the value. Value-TD propagates the goal-reaching signal backward
through exactly those transitions. \B{Direct evidence.} The measure's own
off-diagonal TD residual is $6347$ for the task-blind forward map $\phi(s,a)$
(\texttt{MeasureOnly} --- it never fits its own objective) versus $266$ once the
forward map is task-indexed $\phi(s,a,z)$ (\texttt{fb\_forward\_z}), a $24\times$
better density fit --- yet that cell still scores only $\bad{25.5}$. The occupancy
density is unfittable without a task index, \emph{and fitting it well remains
insufficient for control}: precisely the low-mass-correction gap. The fix is not a
better basis but an end-to-end value-TD coupling of representation, value, and task
--- see the \texttt{fb-modified} note for objectives that put the $\max$ operator
back into a successor measure.

\paragraph{Corollary: the IQL critic is zero-shot.} If the \emph{objective}, not the
representation, is what the measure lacks, the converse should hold --- the IQL
bilinear critic ought to support FB's own zero-shot API. It does. Recovering a task
vector by FB's moment rule from \emph{offline relabelled} transitions,
$z=\mathbb E_\rho[r(s')\,B(s')]$, and conditioning the flow actor on it recovers
\good{95.5} success --- statistically identical to the exact-goal anchor ($95.5$;
$5$ tasks $\times\,3$ seeds). An earlier ``inferred-$z\!\to\!7$'' reading was an
\emph{on-policy sampling} artefact: a mis-conditioned policy rarely reaches the goal,
so its states carry no reward signal to infer from; relabelled \emph{dataset} states
fix it. (The actor consumes the raw $B(g)$, so $z$ must be rescaled to $\|B(g)\|$,
not $\sqrt d$; whitening the non-orthonormal $B$ is refuted --- $\mathbb E[BB^\top]$
is rank-deficient, $\kappa\!\approx\!10^{14}$.) So the IQL representation already
carries FB's task-inference structure: it is the measure \emph{training signal}, not
the bilinear form or the inference API, that FB is missing.

\section{Putting the $\max$ back in the measure: in-sample FB (SG-ISFB)}
\label{sec:isfb}
% ======================================================================
\S\ref{sec:decisions} bundled two things inside D1: the \emph{backup signal}
(occupancy density vs.\ goal-reward) and the \emph{improvement operator} (expectile
$\max$ vs.\ none). Every Direction-B row swaps the \emph{whole} of D1 at once. The
sharper test is to change \emph{only the operator}: keep FB's successor-measure
object, but graft IQL's in-sample $\max$ onto it. \B{If the missing ingredient is
just the $\max$, this should recover the gap; if it is the reward-TD coupling as a
whole, it should not.}

\paragraph{Core insight.} Add a \emph{state-only value forward map} $F_V(s,z)$
(architecturally identical to $F$ but fed a zeroed action), giving a value successor
measure $N_{ij}=\tfrac1{\sqrt d}\langle F_V(s_i,z_i),B(s_j')\rangle$ alongside the
action measure $M_{ij}=\tfrac1{\sqrt d}\langle F(s_i,a_i,z_i),B(s_j')\rangle$.
Project both onto the task to get scalar readouts
$q_i=\tfrac1{\sqrt d}\langle F(s_i,a_i,z_i),z_i\rangle$ (action value) and
$v_i=\tfrac1{\sqrt d}\langle F_V(s_i,z_i),z_i\rangle$ (state value). The improvement
operator is IQL's expectile of $v$ over $q$, $\mathcal L_V=L_2^\tau(\mathrm{sg}\,q-v)$,
with advantage $A_i=\mathrm{sg}(q_i-v_i)$. The one load-bearing change is the
\emph{bootstrap}: the action measure is fit through the \emph{improved value
measure}, $M_{ij}\to\gamma\,\mathrm{sg}\,N^-_{i'j}$ (target net at the next state),
with \B{no $a'\sim\pi$} --- FB's on-policy next-action backup replaced by an
in-sample max'd value measure. Zero-shot readout $z_r=\mathbb E[r(s)B(s)]$ is
untouched, so FB's task inference survives.

\paragraph{Design decisions we tried.} Two ways to push the scalar operator into the
\emph{vector} measure, plus the incidentals that took several passes to get right:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \B{coord} --- apply the expectile coordinatewise to every successor column
  ($N$ as a per-column upper envelope of $M$). Simplest, but $N$ can be assembled
  from mutually \emph{incompatible} per-column argmax actions --- not the occupancy
  of any single policy.
\item \B{scalar-gate} --- apply the $\max$ only to the scalar task value $q$, then
  refit the vector $N$ by advantage-weighted regression toward $\mathrm{sg}\,M$
  using either \B{hard} expectile weights $\tau\mathbb 1[A{>}0]{+}(1{-}\tau)\mathbb
  1[A{\le}0]$ or \B{AWR} weights $\mathrm{clip}(e^{\beta A},0,w_{\max})$. Keeps the
  operator coherent (one action per row) --- the cleaner hybrid on paper.
\item \B{Exact-goal $z_i{=}B(s_i^{\text{future}})$} (removes the multi-task-dilution
  confound; the decisive incidental), \B{goal-success masking} of the measure-TD
  bootstrap (GCIQL-consistent), \B{ortho $\lambda{=}1$} (FB-scale, not cube-$1000$),
  and \B{std-normalized AWR advantage}: the FB scalar $\langle F,z\rangle$
  \emph{inflates without bound} over training ($q$, $A_{\text{std}}$ into the
  thousands as $F$ grows and $B$ stays norm-capped), so a fixed $\beta$ re-saturates
  the $w_{\max}$ clip and AWR collapses; normalizing makes $\beta$ scale-invariant.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Result (cube-single state, 3 seeds @ 500k, torch).}
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l l r@{}}
\toprule
Variant & Operator into the measure & Succ. \\
\midrule
C4 (failed baseline)        & standard FB measure, no operator         & \bad{16.5} \\
plain FB (reference)        & native measure                           & 34 (48 @1M) \\
\B{coord IS-FB}             & coordinatewise expectile envelope        & \B{33.3} \\
SG-ISFB, AWR weights        & scalar gate, smooth $e^{\beta A}$        & 26.7 \\
SG-ISFB, hard weights       & scalar gate, hard expectile              & \bad{8.0} \\
B4 (target)                 & IQL backup on FB skeleton                & \good{87.1} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent\B{What it says.} The operator \emph{helps the measure but nowhere near
enough}. \texttt{coord} roughly \emph{doubles} the C4 floor ($16.5{\to}33$) ---
inserting an in-sample $\max$ into the rebuilt-FB measure is a real lift --- but it
lands right at plain FB's own 500k level ($34$), below even the FB-IQL \emph{import}
band ($59$--$65$), and less than half of B4 ($87$). Two further surprises. \B{(i)}
The scalar-gated refinement, argued to be the cleaner hybrid, \emph{underperforms}
the coordinatewise baseline it was meant to replace ($33{\to}27$ AWR $/\,\bad 8$
hard); on this substrate the incoherent-but-optimistic per-column envelope beats the
coherent scalar gate, and \texttt{hard} weights collapse. \B{(ii)} The measure scale
inflates unbounded ($F$ grows, $B$ norm-capped) --- benign for the scale-invariant
\texttt{coord}/\texttt{hard} operator and the FQL actor, but it is what forced the
AWR std-norm fix and is the standing suspect for the \texttt{hard} collapse.

\noindent In the terms of \S\ref{sec:whatitsays}: SG-ISFB is the
``\emph{improves but plateaus below B4}'' outcome. The $\max$ operator is \emph{not}
the whole of D1 --- putting it back into the successor measure lifts FB off the floor
but does not recover reward-TD's value-controllability. The binding ingredient
remains the end-to-end \emph{reward-TD coupling} of representation, value, and task,
not the improvement operator in isolation. This \emph{sharpens} \S\ref{sec:whatitsays}
rather than softening it: even the one modification \S\ref{sec:whatitsays} flagged as
the promising fix leaves FB in its own band.
