\begin{abstract}
Learning the region successor measure by temporal-difference regression on the
occupancy indicator fails on manipulation play data in a specific way: the
targets are dominated by an action-independent \emph{dwell} term and the critic
converges to the constant that term implies. This note states two
reparameterizations that leave the object and its greedy policy unchanged while
changing what is regressed. \textsc{Delta} regresses the temporal difference of
the indicator; we prove the identity $J=(1-\gamma)M-h(x_0)$, show it preserves
the action $\arg\max$, and show that the estimator's degenerate constant
\emph{relocates} to zero rather than disappearing. \textsc{Part} replaces the
scalar measure by a distribution over a fixed $K$-cell partition fitted by
cross-entropy, making validity structural and supervision dense. On
\texttt{cube-single-play-v0}, \textsc{Delta} exhibits precisely the zero-flux
collapse the analysis predicts, while \textsc{Part} reaches $0.884$ goal success
and transfers zero-shot to point-goal reward families. Both are alternatives to
the multi-step operator of the companion note: that one keeps the target and
lengthens the horizon; these keep the horizon and change the target.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting and the object}

Offline unlabeled ``play'' data $\mathcal{D}$ from OGBench
\citep{park2024ogbench}, state observations. Write $x=\phi_{\mathrm{obj}}(s)$ for
the object coordinates carried by the observation (for \texttt{cube-single}, the
cube position $s_{19:22}$). For a goal $g$ and tolerance $\varepsilon$ define the
\emph{region indicator}
\begin{equation}
h_g(x) \;=\; \ind{\|x-g\|\le\varepsilon} \;\in\;\{0,1\},
\label{eq:indicator}
\end{equation}
extended to a conjunction of blocks with per-block tolerances for multi-object
environments. Under the goal-conditioned policy $\pi(\cdot\mid\cdot,g)$ the
object of interest is the ball-visitation region successor measure
\citep{dayan1993improving,blier2021learning,touati2021learning}
\begin{equation}
M(s,a,g) \;=\; \E\Big[\textstyle\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^{t}\,h_g(x_{t+1})
\;\Big|\; s_0=s,\ a_0=a\Big],
\label{eq:M}
\end{equation}
the expected discounted time spent inside the region, with rewards counted at
the \emph{next} state. Its Bellman equation is
\begin{equation}
M(s,a,g) \;=\; \E\big[\,h_g(x_1) \;+\; \gamma\,M(s_1,a_1,g)\,\big],
\qquad a_1\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s_1,g),
\label{eq:M-bellman}
\end{equation}
and the estimator regresses
$\delta = M(s,a,g) - h_g(x') - \gamma\,\sg[\bar M(s',a',g)]$ against a batch of
columns. Note $M\in[0,\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}]$, which is $[0,100]$ at
$\gamma=0.99$.

\paragraph{The defect.} A trajectory that enters the region at step $T$ and
leaves at $T+L$ contributes $L$ identical labels $h_g=1$ to
\eqref{eq:M-bellman}. The mass of the target is thus governed by the dwell
length $L$, a property of the region and the data rather than of the action
taken at $(s,a)$. The fitted critic settles near the constant $c^{\star}$
solving $c=\E[h]+\gamma c$,
\begin{equation}
c^{\star} \;=\; \frac{\E_{\mathcal{D}}[h]}{1-\gamma},
\label{eq:dwell-constant}
\end{equation}
which is large, well scaled, and action-independent, and from which the actor
extracts nothing. The companion note attacks this by lengthening the horizon of
the operator so the dwell mass enters the \emph{observed} term. The two variants
below instead keep the one-step operator and change what is predicted. Neither
changes $M$.

\section{Variant A (\textsc{Delta}): temporal differencing of the indicator}

\subsection{Definition and the exact identity}

Replace the target reward in \eqref{eq:M} by the temporal difference of the
indicator,
\begin{equation}
J(s,a,g) \;=\; \E\Big[\textstyle\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^{t}
\big(h_g(x_{t+1})-h_g(x_{t})\big)\Big].
\label{eq:J}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Reparameterization]
\label{prop:identity}
For every $(s,a,g)$ and every policy, with both series counted under the
convention of \eqref{eq:M},
\begin{equation}
J(s,a,g) \;=\; (1-\gamma)\,M(s,a,g) \;-\; h_g(x_0),
\qquad x_0=\phi_{\mathrm{obj}}(s).
\label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Split \eqref{eq:J}. The first series is $M$ by definition. For the second,
re-index and use that $x_0$ is deterministic given $s$:
\[
\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^{t}h_g(x_t)
= h_g(x_0)+\sum_{t\ge1}\gamma^{t}h_g(x_t)
= h_g(x_0)+\gamma\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^{t}h_g(x_{t+1}).
\]
Taking expectations the second series equals $h_g(x_0)+\gamma M$, so
$J = M-\big(h_g(x_0)+\gamma M\big) = (1-\gamma)M-h_g(x_0)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Policy invariance]
\label{cor:argmax}
$h_g(x_0)$ depends on $(s,g)$ but not on $a$, and $1-\gamma>0$; hence
$\arg\max_{a} J(s,a,g) = \arg\max_{a} M(s,a,g)$ for every $(s,g)$. $J$ is a
reparameterization of the same object, not a different objective.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Boundedness]
Since $M\in[0,\tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}]$ and $h_g(x_0)\in\{0,1\}$, we have
$(1-\gamma)M\in[0,1]$ and $J\in[-1,1]$, uniformly in $\gamma$.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Estimator}

$J$ obeys the Bellman equation obtained from \eqref{eq:M-bellman} by
substituting the signed reward,
\begin{equation}
J(s,a,g) \;=\; \E\big[\big(h_g(x_1)-h_g(x_0)\big) \;+\; \gamma\,J(s_1,a_1,g)\big],
\label{eq:J-bellman}
\end{equation}
so the estimator differs from the baseline in exactly one term:
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\delta = M - h_g(x') - \gamma\,\sg[\bar M]}_{\text{visitation}}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\underbrace{\delta = M - \big(h_g(x') - h_g(x)\big) - \gamma\,\sg[\bar M]}_{\textsc{delta}} .
\label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
The per-transition target takes four values: $0$ for outside$\,\to\,$outside,
$+1$ on entry, $0$ throughout a dwell, and $-1$ on exit. The counting convention
is load-bearing --- $h_g(x')$ on \texttt{next\_observations} and $h_g(x)$ on
\texttt{observations}. An off-by-one there silently learns a different object
while leaving every loss curve plausible, and is pinned by a regression test.

\subsection{What the differencing does to the supervision}

\begin{lemma}[Dwell telescoping]
\label{lem:telescope}
For a trajectory entering the region at step $T$ and leaving at $T+L$, the
contributions to the two targets are
\[
\underbrace{\gamma^{T}\,\frac{1-\gamma^{L}}{1-\gamma}}_{\text{visitation: }L\text{ labels}}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\underbrace{\gamma^{T}(+1)+\gamma^{T+L}(-1)=\gamma^{T}\big(1-\gamma^{L}\big)}_{\textsc{delta}:\ 2\text{ labels}},
\]
which agree exactly after the factor $(1-\gamma)$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:identity}.
\end{lemma}

The dwell length is therefore not discarded but re-encoded as the discounted
\emph{gap} between an entry and an exit, rather than $L$ repeated identical
labels. The action-independent bulk leaves the regression while the information
it carried is retained.

\subsection{Relocation, not elimination, of the degenerate solution}

\begin{proposition}[Zero-flux constant]
\label{prop:zeroflux}
The constant critic consistent with \eqref{eq:J-bellman} is
\begin{equation}
c^{\star}_{J} \;=\; \frac{\E_{\mathcal{D}}\!\left[\Delta h\right]}{1-\gamma},
\qquad \Delta h = h_g(x')-h_g(x).
\label{eq:zeroflux}
\end{equation}
Over a dataset in which every entry into a region is eventually matched by an
exit, $\E_{\mathcal{D}}[\Delta h]\approx 0$, hence $c^{\star}_{J}\approx0$.
\end{proposition}

Comparing \eqref{eq:dwell-constant} with \eqref{eq:zeroflux}, the lazy solution
does not disappear: it moves. Its diagnostic value changes qualitatively,
though. The visitation constant is large and well scaled and can be mistaken for
a calibrated measure; the delta constant is $\approx0$, so a critic that has
learned nothing announces itself. We pre-registered $\mathrm{msq}=\E[M^{2}]\to0$
with flat loss and behavior-cloning-floor success as the \emph{zero-flux
collapse} signature.

\section{Variant B (\textsc{Part}): fixed-partition simplex measure}

\subsection{Definition}

Fix $K$ prototypes $\{p_k\}_{k=1}^{K}$ drawn from the dataset's object
coordinates and define a soft partition by a per-block scaled radial-basis
softmax,
\begin{equation}
\varphi_k(x) \;=\;
\frac{\exp\!\big(-\tfrac12\sum_{b}\|(x^{(b)}-p_k^{(b)})/\varepsilon_b\|^{2}\big)}
{\sum_{k'}\exp\!\big(-\tfrac12\sum_{b}\|(x^{(b)}-p_{k'}^{(b)})/\varepsilon_b\|^{2}\big)}
\;\in\;\simplex ,
\label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
where $b$ indexes the blocks of the hit specification and $\varepsilon_b$ is that
block's tolerance, so heterogeneous tolerances contribute comparably. The learned
object is the \emph{normalized} occupancy over cells,
\begin{equation}
p^{\star}(s,a,g) \;=\; (1-\gamma)\,\E\Big[\textstyle\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^{t}
\varphi(x_{t+1})\Big] \;\in\;\simplex .
\label{eq:pstar}
\end{equation}
The normalization is exactly the factor of Proposition~\ref{prop:identity}: the
$k$-th coordinate of \eqref{eq:pstar} is $(1-\gamma)M$ integrated against cell
$k$. Since $\sum_k\varphi_k\equiv1$, total mass is conserved by construction and
$p^{\star}$ lies on the simplex with no penalty enforcing it.

\subsection{Estimator}

A network emits $K$ logits from $(s,a,g)$ with $p=\mathrm{softmax}$; the target
mixes the observed cell with the target network's prediction at a bootstrap
action $a^{+}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s',g)$,
\begin{align}
p_{\mathrm{tgt}} &= \sg\big[(1-\gamma)\,\varphi(x') \;+\; \gamma\,\bar p(s',a^{+},g)\big] \;\in\;\simplex,
\label{eq:ptgt}\\
\mathcal{L} &= -\,\E\Big[\textstyle\sum_{k} p_{\mathrm{tgt},k}\,\log p_k\Big].
\label{eq:ce}
\end{align}
Both terms of \eqref{eq:ptgt} lie on $\simplex$ and the weights sum to one, so
$p_{\mathrm{tgt}}\in\simplex$ and \eqref{eq:ce} is a proper cross-entropy between
distributions. Two properties follow that the scalar estimator lacks: validity
($p\ge0$, $\sum_k p_k=1$) is structural rather than penalized, and every
transition supplies a dense $K$-vector target instead of a mostly-zero scalar.

\subsection{Control and zero-shot reward evaluation}

The actor maximizes the mass its own goal's cell receives,
\begin{equation}
q(s,a,g) \;=\; p(s,a,g)^{\top} z_g,
\qquad z_g = \sg\big[\varphi(g)\big],
\label{eq:actor}
\end{equation}
evaluated at frozen head parameters so that no actor gradient reaches the
measure. By \eqref{eq:pstar}, $q=(1-\gamma)\,M\cdot z_g$ up to the
action-independent level $\varphi(x_0)^{\top}z_g$, whence a centered normalizer is
required.

For an arbitrary reward $r$ presented at test time, evaluate it at the prototypes
and read the value off with no retraining:
\begin{equation}
z_{r,k} = r(p_k),
\qquad
F^{\Delta} = p - \varphi(x_0),
\qquad
Q_r(s,a) \;=\; \frac{r(x_0) + F^{\Delta\top} z_r}{1-\gamma}.
\label{eq:qr}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:qr} is the variant-B analogue of \eqref{eq:identity}: the
$(1-\gamma)^{-1}$ and the $-\varphi(x_0)$ are the same normalization and the same
action-independent offset, in vector form.

\paragraph{Conversion for variant A.} A checkpoint trained under
\eqref{eq:residual} represents $J$, not $M$; any calibration or reward readout
must invert \eqref{eq:identity} first,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{M} \;=\; \frac{h_g(x_0)+\widehat{J}}{1-\gamma}.
\label{eq:convert}
\end{equation}
For a composite task vector $z_r=\sum_j w_j\psi(g_j)$ the correct offset is the
same-weights hit sum $\sum_j w_j h_{g_j}(x_0)$, by linearity of
\eqref{eq:identity} in the columns. By Corollary~\ref{cor:argmax} the conversion
does not change action selection; it is required for \emph{quoting} $\widehat M$.

\section{Empirical status}

Both variants were run on \texttt{cube-single-play-v0}, three seeds, $500$k
gradient steps, trained from scratch, otherwise identical to the standing
recipe. Goal success is the environment's own evaluation over its five tasks at
$50$ episodes; the zero-shot columns apply \eqref{eq:convert} for \textsc{Delta}
and \eqref{eq:qr} for \textsc{Part}.

\paragraph{Reading.} \textsc{Delta} follows Proposition~\ref{prop:zeroflux}
exactly: $\mathrm{msq}=\E[M^2]$ falls from $0.34$--$1.70$ at $25$k to
$0.0042$--$0.0100$ at $500$k --- two to three orders of magnitude toward zero ---
while the event rate $\E|\Delta h|$ holds flat at $0.0011$ against a level
$\E[h]=0.0148$. The critic converges to the relocated constant. Temporal
centering therefore relocates the degenerate solution and makes it legible, but
does not by itself yield a usable measure.

\textsc{Part} passes its pre-registered gate (success $\ge0.5$ and entropy
$<0.9\log K=4.99$) on every seed and transfers zero-shot to point-goal reward
families while degrading on extended regions. The contrast is informative
because both variants share the same underlying object: what differs is the
\emph{parameterization} of the prediction --- a normalized distribution over
cells versus an unbounded scalar --- and the density of supervision, not the
semantics of occupancy.

\paragraph{Caveat.} The wave contains no behavior-cloning-only control, so
\textsc{Part}'s $0.884$ clears its gate but is not yet attributable to the
measure term rather than the cloning term.

\paragraph{Relation to the multi-step note.} The companion note keeps the
indicator target and lengthens the operator's horizon, moving the dwell mass into
the observed term; the variants here keep the one-step operator and change the
predicted quantity. The two are rival explanations of the same collapse ---
starved bootstrap versus level-dominated regression --- and are designed to be
run head-to-head in identical slots.
