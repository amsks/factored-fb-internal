\begin{abstract}
Successor-measure methods (FB and relatives) learn the discounted state-occupancy
of a policy by one-step temporal-difference (TD) learning. On manipulation
\emph{play} data, occupancy over goal-relevant object coordinates is dominated by
\emph{dwell}: objects rest at stable configurations for tens to hundreds of steps
between brief, action-driven transports. We show that one-step TD is structurally
mismatched to this regime---its fixed point under sparse anchoring degenerates to
the action-independent dwell constant---and derive a multi-step (\(k\)-step /
\(\lambda\)-return) measure-TD operator whose error decomposition is
\emph{gated by the data's interaction sparsity}: the off-policy bias of the
observed \(k\)-step window is bounded by \(\gamma^{T_{\mathrm{int}}}\), where
\(T_{\mathrm{int}}\) is the time to the first interaction with the tracked
object. Play data thus licenses long observed returns precisely where one-step
bootstrapping fails. The dwell scale sets the lower edge of the useful horizon
and the discount half-life the upper; a geometric horizon recovers the
\(\lambda\)-return with a strictly better contraction modulus and no
dataset-specific constant. The data are untouched: the structure enters through
the estimator only.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}

Let \((\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,\gamma)\) be an MDP with \(\gamma\in(0,1)\).
A known projection \(c:\mathcal{S}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_c}\) extracts the
goal-relevant object coordinate (e.g.\ cube position). For a goal \(g\) and
radius \(\varepsilon\), write \(X=B_\varepsilon(g)\) and
\(h(s)=\ind{c(s)\in X}\). Data are collected by a behavior policy \(\beta\); we
seek the successor measure of a target policy \(\pi\),
\begin{equation}
\M^\pi(s,a,X)\;=\;\E^\pi\!\Big[\textstyle\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\gamma^{\,t-1}h(s_t)\;\Big|\;s_0=s,\,a_0=a\Big],
\label{eq:measure}
\end{equation}
counting from the next state and bounded by \((1-\gamma)^{-1}\). Evaluating
\(\M^\pi\) against a reward \(r\) supported on object coordinates yields
\(Q^\pi_r(s,a)=\int \M^\pi(s,a,\mathrm{d}x)\,r(x)\), the identity underlying
zero-shot readout in FB-style methods.

\paragraph{Empirical regime (measured, not assumed).}
Across eleven manipulation datasets (OGBench cube single/double/triple/quadruple
and scene; Metaworld play-resample in two regimes and two tasks; two expert
sets), region occupancy factors as
\(\text{mass}\approx P(\text{arrive})\cdot\E[\gamma^{T_{\mathrm{arr}}}]\cdot
\frac{1-\gamma^{L}}{1-\gamma}\)
with log-correlation \(\ge 0.91\), where \(L\) is the in-region sojourn length;
the single longest sojourn carries \(74\)--\(99\%\) of in-region time; and mean
sojourns grow from \(22\) steps (cube-single) to \(140\) steps
(cube-quadruple). Object coordinates are exactly stationary on
\(48\)--\(92\%\) of transitions. We refer to this two-timescale,
piecewise-constant structure as \emph{dwell structure}.

\section{The \texorpdfstring{$k$}{k}-step Bellman operator for measures}

Splitting the sum in \eqref{eq:measure} at time \(k\ge 1\),
\begin{equation}
\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\gamma^{t-1}h(s_t)
=\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{k}\gamma^{t-1}h(s_t)}_{\text{observed}}
\;+\;\gamma^{k}\sum_{u=1}^{\infty}\gamma^{u-1}h(s_{k+u}),
\end{equation}
and taking expectations under \(\pi\) gives, for every \(k\),
\begin{equation}
\M^\pi(s,a,X)=(\Tk \M^\pi)(s,a,X)
\;\mathrel{{:}{=}}\;
\E^\pi\!\Big[\sum_{t=1}^{k}\gamma^{t-1}h(s_t)\Big]
+\gamma^{k}\,\E^\pi\big[\M^\pi(s_k,a_k,X)\big].
\label{eq:kstep}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Contraction and fixed point]
\label{prop:contraction}
For any bounded \(\M,\M'\),
\(\|\Tk \M-\Tk \M'\|_\infty\le\gamma^{k}\|\M-\M'\|_\infty\).
Hence \(\Tk\) is a \(\gamma^k\)-contraction whose unique fixed point is
\(\M^\pi\) for every \(k\ge1\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The observed term is \(\M\)-independent; the bootstrap terms differ by
\(\gamma^k\E^\pi[\M-\M']\le\gamma^k\|\M-\M'\|_\infty\). Uniqueness by Banach;
\eqref{eq:kstep} shows \(\M^\pi\) is a fixed point.
\end{proof}

The choice of \(k\) therefore never changes the target, only the error
dynamics: \(k{=}1\) is standard measure TD, \(k\to\infty\) is Monte-Carlo
occupancy counting.

\section{Error decomposition and the off-policy bias}

Write a learned measure as \(\widehat{\M}=\M^\pi+e\). Applying the
\emph{empirical} operator---trajectory actions from \(\beta\), bootstrap action
from \(\pi\)---the post-update error decomposes as
\begin{align}
e' \;&=\;
\underbrace{\gamma^{k}\,\E\big[e(s_k,a_k)\big]}_{\text{bootstrap error,}\ \gamma^k\text{-shrunk}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\Delta_k^{\beta\pi}}_{\text{off-policy bias}}
\;+\;\text{sampling noise},
\label{eq:bias}\\
\Delta_k^{\beta\pi}\;&=\;
\E^{\beta}\!\Big[\sum_{t=1}^{k}\gamma^{t-1}h(s_t)\Big]-
\E^{\pi}\!\Big[\sum_{t=1}^{k}\gamma^{t-1}h(s_t)\Big].\nonumber
\end{align}
\(\Delta_1^{\beta\pi}=0\) since the first transition is conditioned on
\((s,a)\); generically \(|\Delta_k|\) grows with \(k\)---the classical argument
for small \(k\). Dwell structure breaks that argument.

\begin{assumption}[Interaction-gated object dynamics]
\label{ass:contact}
There is an interaction indicator \(\iota(s,a)\in\{0,1\}\) (contact with the
tracked object) such that \(c(s_{t+1})=c(s_t)\) whenever
\(\iota(s_t,a_t)=0\).
\end{assumption}

This is the measured piecewise-constancy above (exact to numerical precision in
the simulators considered). Let
\(T_{\mathrm{int}}=\min\{t\ge1:\iota(s_t,a_t)=1\}\)
be the first interaction time after \((s_0,a_0)\).

\begin{lemma}[Dwell invariance]
\label{lem:dwell}
On the event \(\{k\le T_{\mathrm{int}}\}\), the observed sum is
policy-independent:
\(\sum_{t=1}^{k}\gamma^{t-1}h(s_t)=h(s_1)\,\frac{1-\gamma^{k}}{1-\gamma}\),
a function of the initial object position only. Consequently
\begin{equation}
\big|\Delta_k^{\beta\pi}\big|\;\le\;
\E^{\beta}\Big[\gamma^{\,T_{\mathrm{int}}}\Big]\cdot
\frac{1-\gamma^{\,(k-T_{\mathrm{int}})^+}}{1-\gamma}.
\label{eq:dwellbound}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
While \(t<T_{\mathrm{int}}\), Assumption~\ref{ass:contact} freezes
\(c(s_t)=c(s_1)\), hence \(h(s_t)=h(s_1)\) regardless of the actions taken;
the partial sum is the stated geometric series under \emph{every} policy.
Policies can disagree only at \(t>T_{\mathrm{int}}\); bounding the disagreement
of the remaining \((k-T_{\mathrm{int}})^+\) terms by the maximal indicator sum
and discounting by \(\gamma^{T_{\mathrm{int}}}\) yields \eqref{eq:dwellbound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Where the bound bites]
The bias is gated by \(\E[\gamma^{T_{\mathrm{int}}}]\): the discount decays the
window before the policies can disagree about the object. Measured interaction
gaps give \(\gamma^{T_{\mathrm{int}}}\!\approx\!0.4\)--\(0.55\) on cube-single
(\(T_{\mathrm{int}}\!\sim\!60\)--\(90\)) and \(\approx\!0.17\) on cube-double
(\(T_{\mathrm{int}}\!\sim\!176\)): the safe-horizon budget is \emph{largest} on
the multi-object environments where one-step TD fails hardest. In the opposite
regime (navigation: the agent is the object, \(\iota\equiv1\),
\(T_{\mathrm{int}}=1\)) the bound is vacuous and small-\(k\) orthodoxy applies.
The admissible horizon is itself a function of the data's dwell structure.
\end{remark}

\section{Choosing the horizon}

\paragraph{Lower edge: the dwell scale.}
For a rest region with sojourn length \(L\) and arrival time
\(T_{\mathrm{arr}}\), the observed part of the target carries
\(\E[G^{(k)}_{\mathrm{obs}}]\approx
P(T_{\mathrm{arr}}\!\le\!k)\cdot\frac{1-\gamma^{\min(L,\,k-T_{\mathrm{arr}})}}{1-\gamma}\)
while the bootstrap carries weight \(\gamma^{k}\). For \(k\ll L\) the dwell
mass \(\frac{1-\gamma^{L}}{1-\gamma}\) (\(\approx\!43\) at \(L{=}56\),
\(\gamma{=}0.99\)) sits inside the bootstrap---the quantity TD must
reconstruct by iterating on itself. At \(k{=}1\) the observed signal fires at
the one-step hit rate (\(0.05\%\) per sample on cube-double regions). For
\(k\gtrsim L\) the full sojourn mass moves into the observed, policy-invariant
part. Hence \(k\) should exceed the median sojourn (\(22\)--\(60\) steps on our
datasets).

\paragraph{Upper edge: the discount half-life.}
Past \(k^\ast=\ln 2/\ln(1/\gamma)\approx 69\) steps (\(\gamma{=}0.99\)),
\(\gamma^{k}\) has halved and further observed steps contribute
\(O(\gamma^{k})\) mass while adding variance; the useful range is
\(k\in[L_{\mathrm{med}},\,\sim\!2k^\ast]\approx[50,150]\), fixed by two
measurable quantities rather than tuned.

\paragraph{No fixed \(k\): the \(\lambda\)-return.}
Drawing \(k\sim\mathrm{Geom}(1-\lambda)\) per update yields the expected
operator
\begin{equation}
\Tlam=(1-\lambda)\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda^{\,n-1}\,\mathcal{T}_n ,
\end{equation}
the classical \(\lambda\)-return for measures.

\begin{proposition}[Modulus of \(\Tlam\)]
\(\Tlam\) is a contraction with modulus
\((1-\lambda)\sum_{n\ge1}\lambda^{n-1}\gamma^{n}
=\frac{\gamma(1-\lambda)}{1-\lambda\gamma}\;<\;\gamma\)
for every \(\lambda\in(0,1)\), with on-policy fixed point \(\M^\pi\) for all
\(\lambda\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Mix Proposition~\ref{prop:contraction} over the geometric weights; the series
sums to the stated modulus. The fixed point is shared by every \(\mathcal{T}_n\).
\end{proof}

Off-policy without corrections, the fixed point of the mixed empirical operator
interpolates between \(\M^\beta\) (\(\lambda\to1\)) and the one-step
off-policy solution (\(\lambda\to0\)); Lemma~\ref{lem:dwell} applies to each
\(\mathcal{T}_n\) inside the mixture, so the interpolation error is again gated
by \(\gamma^{T_{\mathrm{int}}}\). Setting the mean horizon
\((1-\lambda)^{-1}\approx(1-\gamma)^{-1}\) matches the mixture to the discount
and removes the per-dataset constant. Truncation at an episode boundary
replaces the tail with the \(\gamma^{k}\)-weighted bootstrap and preserves the
operator.

\section{Contrast: the one-step collapse}

At \(k{=}1\), \(\mathcal{T}_1\M=\E[h(s_1)]+\gamma\E[\M(s_1,a_1)]\). For a
sparse region, \(\E[h(s_1)]\approx0\) on almost every sample, so the empirical
operator is approximately \(\gamma\times\)(a projection of \(\M\)'s own
average). Any near-constant function \(C\) satisfies
\(\mathcal{T}_1 C\approx\bar p+\gamma C\) with \(\bar p\) the dataset-average
one-step hit rate, whose stable point \(C^\ast=\bar p/(1-\gamma)\) is the
dataset-average mass of the region---the dwell-implied constant. This is the
degenerate fixed point we observe empirically on cube-double (measured mass
\(43.5\) vs.\ dwell-implied \(43.0\), with no state--action variation): starved
of anchors, one-step TD converges to the mass \emph{level} and loses the
\emph{ranking}. Under \(\Tk\) with \(k\gtrsim L\), the anchor probability per
update grows from \(P(\text{hit in one step})\) to
\(P(\text{hit within }k)\), and the constant arrives pre-assembled in the
observed term; the degenerate fixed point loses its basin.

\section{Estimator, not data}

No preprocessing is performed: no event detection, no trajectory segmentation,
no relabeling into aligned sub-demonstrations. The sampler reads raw
length-\(k\) windows from stored trajectories (the same access pattern used by
hindsight goal sampling), and the structure enters exclusively through
(i) the multi-step operator above, and (ii) optionally a structured measure head
\(\M(s,a,g)=D(g)\cdot H(s,a,g)\) with \(D\ge0\) a goal-only magnitude and
\(H\in[0,1]\) a bounded bilinear factor \(\phi(s,a)^{\!\top}\psi(g)\), which
gives the action-independent dwell magnitude a dedicated low-capacity container
(a dueling-style decomposition for measures). The zero-shot readout
\(z_r=\E_{\rho}[\psi(g)D(g)r(g)]\), \(Q_r=\phi^{\top}z_r\) is unchanged from FB.

\subsection*{Pre-registered empirical gates (to be run)}
(1) On cube-double phase-2 conditions, the learned mass must \emph{not} pin to
the dwell constant, and signed state--action variation must survive.
(2) Performance should be flat in \(k\) once \(k\gtrsim L_{\mathrm{med}}\) and
degrade only as \(k\to1\); sharp tuning in \(k\) falsifies the mechanism as
stated. (3) Calibration is scored against the \(\lambda\)-return fixed point,
not the one-step one, when \(\lambda>0\).
