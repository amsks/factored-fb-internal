\begin{abstract}
We learn the successor measure as a \emph{normalized probability density} over object
configurations rather than as an unbounded scalar per goal. A two-tower score
$F(s,a,g)^\top B(u)/\tau$ is softmax-normalized over a query set redrawn from the data at
every update, and fit by cross-entropy to the one-step target
$(1-\gamma)\kappa(x') + \gamma\bar p$. Non-negativity and unit mass are then structural,
so the method carries none of the machinery that previous successor-measure cells in this
project needed and that each measured as harmful: no orthonormalization, no mass anchor,
no scale normalization, no two-phase curriculum. It trains from scratch in one stage.
On OGBench manipulation it reaches $0.929$ on cube-single and $0.736$ on cube-double,
the latter $+0.19$ over the best prior method on that environment and roughly
$3\times$ goal-conditioned IQL, against a measured behavior-cloning floor of $0.019$.
\end{abstract}

\section{The object}

Let $x=\phi_{\mathrm{obj}}(s)$ be the object coordinates carried by the observation and
$\gamma$ the discount. For a policy $\pi(\cdot\mid\cdot,g)$ the successor measure is the
discounted occupancy of configuration sets,
\begin{equation}
M(\cdot\mid s,a,g) \;=\; \E\Big[\textstyle\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^{t}\,\delta_{x_{t+1}}\Big],
\label{eq:M}
\end{equation}
counted at the NEXT state. Its total mass is $1/(1-\gamma)$ for every $(s,a,g)$, so
\begin{equation}
\dnu(\cdot\mid s,a,g) \;=\; (1-\gamma)\,M(\cdot\mid s,a,g)
\label{eq:nu}
\end{equation}
is a \emph{probability measure} over object configurations: where the discounted future
time goes. Equations \eqref{eq:M} and \eqref{eq:nu} carry the same information; the
difference is entirely in what a network is asked to output, and that difference is the
method.

\paragraph{Why the normalization matters here.} The unnormalized form has a degenerate
solution that fits the data well: a constant $c^\star=\E_\mathcal{D}[h]/(1-\gamma)$ solves
the scalar Bellman equation for an indicator reward $h$, is large, well-scaled, and
action-independent, and yields a critic from which no policy can be extracted. Every
failed occupancy cell in this project converged there. Under \eqref{eq:nu} the analogous
degenerate answer is the uniform distribution, which a cross-entropy objective against a
peaked target penalizes rather than rewards.

\section{Parameterization}

A finite partition of configuration space (a previous variant of this work used $K=256$
dataset-drawn prototypes) makes \eqref{eq:nu} representable but fixes the support in
advance. We instead score arbitrary queries with two towers,
\begin{equation}
f(s,a,g,u) \;=\; F(s,a,g)^{\top} B(\tilde u)\,/\,\tau ,
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde u$ denotes per-block $\varepsilon$-scaled coordinates so that environments
with heterogeneous tolerances contribute comparably. Given a query set
$U=\{u_j\}_{j=1}^{M}$ the predicted distribution is
\begin{equation}
w_j(s,a,g) \;=\; \mathrm{softmax}_j\, f(s,a,g,u_j) .
\label{eq:w}
\end{equation}
$F$ is two-headed; $B$ is single, since it is a feature map of a configuration alone.

\paragraph{The queries are resampled, not fixed.} $U$ is redrawn at every update from the
object marginal --- in round 1, the batch's own next-state object coordinates, so $M$
equals the batch size. Consequently $M$ is a \emph{Monte-Carlo sample size}, not a spatial
resolution: increasing it reduces integration error rather than changing the represented
space, evaluation may use a larger pool than training, and no prototype artifact needs to
be stored alongside the checkpoint. Empirically the readout is under-converged at
$M{=}4096$ and converged by $16384$ (per-task drift $0.000$ from $16384$ to $32768$),
while the induced \emph{ordering} is insensitive to $M$ ($0.8450$ vs $0.8448$).

\section{Estimator}

With $\kappa(\cdot\mid x')$ an $\varepsilon$-kernel mollifier around the observed next
state, evaluated at the query points, and $\bar p$ the target towers' distribution at the
bootstrap action $a^{+}\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s',g)$,
\begin{align}
t &= \sg\big[(1-\gamma)\,\kappa(\cdot\mid x') \;+\; \gamma\,\bar p(s',a^{+},g)\big],
\label{eq:target}\\
\mathcal{L} &= -\,\E\Big[\textstyle\sum_j t_j\,\log w_j\Big].
\label{eq:ce}
\end{align}
Both terms of \eqref{eq:target} lie on the simplex and their weights sum to one, so $t$ is
a probability vector and \eqref{eq:ce} is a proper cross-entropy: a projection of one
distribution onto another, not a regression onto an unbounded scalar.

Two properties follow that the scalar estimator lacks. Validity ($w\ge0$, $\sum_j w_j=1$)
is structural, so nothing has to be added to enforce it. And supervision is dense: every
transition supplies a full $M$-vector target, where the indicator reward of the scalar form
is non-zero on roughly $1.5\%$ of transitions on cube-single and far less for off-diagonal
goals on conjunctive environments.

\paragraph{Control.} The actor maximizes the mass its own goal receives,
$q(s,a,g)=w(s,a,g)^{\top}\kappa(\cdot\mid g)$, evaluated at frozen head parameters so no
actor gradient reaches the measure. Because $q$ contains an action-independent level, a
centered normalizer is required.

\paragraph{Zero-shot reward evaluation.} For a reward $r$ presented at test time, evaluate
it at the query pool and take an inner product,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{Q}_r(s,a) \;=\; \frac{r(x_0) + \big(w - \kappa(\cdot\mid x_0)\big)^{\top} r(U)}{1-\gamma},
\label{eq:readout}
\end{equation}
which is the classical successor-representation identity --- occupancy dotted with reward
--- recovered in a continuous space. \eqref{eq:readout} is exactly linear in $r$.

\section{What this removes}

Each of the following was a component of previous successor-measure cells in this project,
and each was independently measured to be harmful or unnecessary once \eqref{eq:nu} is the
learned object.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Orthonormalization.} Removing it took cube-single first-passage from $0.225$
to $0.967$. Density has no scale degeneracy for it to fix.
\item \textbf{Mass anchors and non-negativity hinges.} A campaign on measure validity found
the mass anchor itself responsible for an ordering loss (Spearman $.68\to.48$). Under a
softmax, mass conservation is not something to enforce.
\item \textbf{Scale normalization.} Introduced to stop unbounded $\langle F,z\rangle$
inflation from destabilizing a shared critic; a normalized output cannot inflate.
\item \textbf{The two-phase curriculum.} The ball-visitation objective does not train from
scratch, which is why a first-passage pretraining stage and a warm start existed. Density
trains from scratch, so there is no phase switch, no frozen backward map, and no
regression between phases to explain.
\item \textbf{Hindsight test-point columns.} The one repair that rescued the two-phase
program on conjunctive environments. Density does not use them.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Reading.} The cube-double column is the informative one. Cube-single separates
almost nothing --- the cloning floor is $.111$ and four methods cluster near the top --- so
density's $.929$ there was, on its own, weak evidence. On cube-double, where the region is
a two-block conjunction and the two-phase program lost success moving from phase 1 to phase
2, density trained from scratch exceeds every prior arm by a wide margin. The behavior-only
control at $.019$ establishes that essentially none of this is the cloning term.

\paragraph{Diagnostics.} Health is stable across seeds (cross-entropy $2.86$--$2.91$,
median effective support ${\approx}9.3$--$9.7$ of $M{=}1024$ on cube-double). The
self-mass $\kappa$ at the row's own successor is ${\approx}.057$ on cube-single and
${\approx}.51$ on cube-double: a six-dimensional conjunctive space separates points more at
the same $\varepsilon$, so the same kernel is effectively sharper. Calibration against
Monte-Carlo region masses passes the pre-registered Spearman gate ($\ge.65$) on the
checkpoints tested, with the honest caveat that ordering is strong at coarse scale
($.83$--$.85$ at $4\varepsilon$) and only moderate at the native $\varepsilon$
($.41$--$.48$).

\section{Limitations}

Scene is unresolved at the time of writing; its partial trajectory shows a stark and
reproducible split, with three of five tasks near-solved and two at zero, which is a
specific signature to explain rather than an average to quote. The zero-shot reward-family
comparison against the two-phase measure is valid on multi-object environments but not on
cube-single, where the two evaluation harnesses draw their region rewards differently ---
a trap that produces an apparent $2.5\times$ gap out of two different reward sets. Finally,
the query set in round 1 is the batch's own successors; whether an independent or
replay-based query pool matters on sparser conjunctive regions is untested.
