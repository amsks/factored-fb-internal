\section{Setting}
\label{sec:prelim}

The successor measure of a policy $\pi$ is its discounted future-state occupancy,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sm}
M^\pi(s,a,X) = \E_\pi\!\left[ \sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^t \ind{s_{t+1}\in X} \;\middle|\; s_0=s,a_0=a \right],
\qquad
Q_r^\pi(s,a) = \int r(x)\,dM^\pi(s,a,x),
\end{equation}
so one learned measure evaluates any downstream reward \citep{dayan1993improving,blier2021learning}.
Forward-backward (FB) representations \citep{touati2021learning,touati2023does} factorize its density against the dataset marginal $\rho$, $m_z(s,a,x) = F(s,a,z)^\top B(x)$, embed a reward as $z_r \propto \E_{x\sim\rho}[r(x)B(x)]$, and read values as $Q_r \approx F(s,a,z_r)^\top z_r$.
The standard estimator is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fb-loss}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FB}} = \tfrac12 \E_{x\sim\rho} \left[ (m_z-\gamma\bar m_z)^2 \right] - \E \left[ m_z(s,a,s') - \gamma\bar m_z(s,a,s') \right] + \lambda_{\mathrm{ortho}} \mathcal L_{\mathrm{ortho}}:
\end{equation}
random queries get a squared residual; the observed successor gets a linear positive term.

\section{Why stationary segments dominate the FB objective}
\label{sec:play}

Manipulation play alternates brief object-changing interactions with long periods at persistent configurations: approach, grasp, transport, release, then rest.
On \texttt{cube-single-play-v0} the cube is stationary on $48\%$ of transitions and stationary runs have median length $44$ steps; after entering a $4$\,cm region a trajectory accumulates $25$--$31$ discounted visits there.
On \texttt{cube-double} each cube is stationary on $72\%$ of transitions; on \texttt{scene}, drawer and window on $91\%$ and buttons on $99.6\%$ (Figure~\ref{fig:playdata}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{figures/playdata.png}
\caption{\emph{Left:} three \texttt{cube-single} play trajectories. \emph{Right:} discounted occupancy accumulated after first entering a $4$\,cm goal region. Most successor mass accumulates after arrival.}
\label{fig:playdata}
\end{figure}

This structure repeatedly reinforces the same successor.
A rest of length $L$ contributes $L$ near-identical positive terms, one per transition, whereas the transition that brought the cube there appears only once; a $44$-step rest therefore gives the same configuration roughly forty opportunities to influence the objective.
Repeated stationary transitions create two problems.
First, the linear positive term in Equation~\ref{eq:fb-loss} can increase the scale of the critic without bound.
Second, when the successor target is a region indicator $h(s',g)=\ind{s'\in B_\epsilon(g)}$, the Bellman objective admits an action-independent constant solution $c^\star = \E_\rho[h]/(1-\gamma)$.
In either case, the objective can be reduced without learning which actions move the object.

A frozen-representation experiment separates the objective from representation capacity (Figure~\ref{fig:divergence}).
With a backward representation frozen from a checkpoint supporting $0.97$ task success, the standard FB objective grows the critic scale from $154$ to $5.4\times10^4$ within $65$k steps and success falls to $0.42$; orthonormalization bounds the scale but caps success at $0.225$.
A capable representation therefore does not rescue the objective: the instability follows the estimator.

\section{\DensityFB: soft and hard variants}
\label{sec:method}

We consider a goal-conditioned policy $\pi(a\mid s,g)$ and write $M(\cdot\mid s,a,g)$ for its successor measure, restricted to the goal-relevant object coordinates $x=\phi_{\mathrm{obj}}(s)$ (the pushforward of the state successor measure, which is exact for any reward defined on object coordinates).
Since the total discounted mass is $1/(1-\gamma)$ for every $(s,a)$, multiplying by $1-\gamma$ yields a probability distribution:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nu}
\nu(\cdot\mid s,a,g) = (1-\gamma)\, M(\cdot\mid s,a,g).
\end{equation}
The key change is that $F^\top B$ becomes a score rather than an unconstrained density: a softmax over sampled query configurations turns the scores into a normalized distribution,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:density-softmax}
f(s,a,g,u) = F(s,a,g)^\top B(\tilde u)/\tau,
\qquad
w_j(s,a,g) = \frac{\exp f(s,a,g,u_j)}{\sum_k \exp f(s,a,g,u_k)},
\end{equation}
over a query set $U=\{u_j\}$ redrawn from the dataset marginal at every update (the batch's own next object configurations).
Non-negativity and unit mass hold by construction, and the query count is a Monte-Carlo sample size, not a discretization: the checkpoint stores only $F$ and $B$.

Each update asks one question: how should one unit of normalized future occupancy be distributed across candidate object configurations?
The target mixes an immediate distribution around the observed successor with the bootstrapped prediction, and is fit by cross-entropy:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:density-loss}
t = \sg \left[ (1-\gamma)\, \kappa(\cdot\mid x') + \gamma\, \bar w(s',a^+,g) \right],
\qquad
\mathcal L = - \E \Big[ \sum_j t_j \log w_j \Big],
\qquad a^+ \sim \pi_{\bar\omega}(\cdot\mid s',g),
\end{equation}
where $\bar w$ is the target-network prediction and $\pi_{\bar\omega}$ is the target copy of the goal-conditioned actor introduced below.
The first term places $1-\gamma$ of the probability mass around the observed next configuration; the remaining $\gamma$ is inherited from the next state's predicted occupancy.
Both terms lie on the simplex, so $t$ is itself a distribution.
We evaluate two immediate targets, differing only in how the immediate mass is assigned:
\begin{align}
\text{soft:}\quad &\kappa_\epsilon(u_j\mid x') \propto \exp\!\left( -\tfrac12 \|(u_j-x')/\epsilon\|^2 \right),
\label{eq:kappa-soft}\\
\text{hard:}\quad &\kappa_j(x'_i) = \ind{j = i},
\label{eq:kappa-hard}
\end{align}
with $\epsilon$ the task tolerance.
The hard target is an index-level one-hot: $U$ is built from the batch's next configurations, so row $i$'s successor occupies slot $i$.
This keeps the target on the simplex even when several rows share the same object coordinates, which stationary segments make common; a coordinate-equality indicator would place mass greater than one on duplicates.

This removes the inflation without deleting dwell mass.
A long rest can make the predicted occupancy concentrate at that configuration, as it should, but it cannot increase the total amount of successor mass; the action-independent solution becomes the uniform distribution, which cross-entropy penalizes against any concentrated target and which is visible in the prediction's entropy.
Hard and soft differ only in where the immediate mass goes: hard puts it on the realized successor, soft distributes it over the successor's $\epsilon$-neighborhood.

\paragraph{Fixed point and contraction.}
Normalization does not change the underlying successor fixed point: for the hard variant it only rescales the measure by $1-\gamma$, so the Bellman operator retains the same fixed-point information while constraining total mass; the soft variant additionally smooths the immediate successor over an $\epsilon$-neighborhood, so its fixed point is the correspondingly smoothed measure.
Writing $\mathcal T\nu \,(\cdot\mid s,a) = (1-\gamma)\,\E[\kappa(\cdot\mid x_1)] + \gamma\,\E_{s_1,a^+}\left[ \nu(\cdot\mid s_1,a^+) \right]$ for the operator whose sample version is Equation~\ref{eq:density-loss}, for any two conditional measures
\begin{equation}
\sup_{s,a}\, \big\| \mathcal T\nu_1 - \mathcal T\nu_2 \big\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\;\le\; \gamma\, \sup_{s,a}\, \big\| \nu_1 - \nu_2 \big\|_{\mathrm{TV}},
\end{equation}
since the immediate term cancels and the expectation is an average.
$\mathcal T$ is therefore a $\gamma$-contraction in total variation with the ($\epsilon$-smoothed, for the soft variant) normalized successor measure as its unique fixed point, exactly as the scalar Bellman operator underlying FB is a $\gamma$-contraction with $M$ as its fixed point.
What differs between the methods is only the projection under function approximation: FB projects in squared error onto the bilinear class, where the play-induced inflation of Section~\ref{sec:play} lives, while \DensityFB{} projects in cross-entropy onto softmax distributions, where scale is not a degree of freedom.

For control, the actor maximizes the predicted mass near its goal, $q(s,a,g) = w(s,a,g)^\top \kappa(\cdot\mid g)$, computed at frozen measure parameters, inside the standard flow behavior-cloning objective:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:actor}
\mathcal L_\pi = \lambda_{\mathrm{BC}}\, \mathcal L_{\mathrm{BC\text{-}flow}} \;-\; \E\!\left[ \frac{ q(s, \pi(s,g), g) }{ s_q } \right],
\qquad s_q = \E\big[\,|q|\,\big],\ \lambda_{\mathrm{BC}} = 3,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{BC\text{-}flow}}$ is the flow-matching behavior-cloning loss and $s_q$ is the usual magnitude normalizer (centered, since $q$ carries an action-independent level).
The behavior-cloning row in Table~\ref{tab:main} trains the same actor with the $q$ term removed.

\label{sec:main-results}

\section{Reward evaluation}
\label{sec:reward-transfer}

The reward-transfer experiment asks a different question from goal reaching: does the learned object represent only the five benchmark goals, or can it be integrated against a new reward?
At test time we approximate the integral of $r$ against the learned successor distribution with a pool $U_P$ of $16{,}384$ object configurations drawn from the dataset: we evaluate $r$ on the pool once and take its expectation under the predicted occupancy $w$, which gives $w^\top r(U_P)/(1-\gamma)$.
One correction improves this estimate.
Let $x_0$ be the current state's object configuration, which need not appear in $U_P$, and let $\kappa(\cdot\mid x_0)$ be its kernel weights over the pool.
The finite pool and the kernel smooth the reward, and the smoothing error is largest around $x_0$, where the predicted occupancy concentrates at small $t$.
We therefore replace the smoothed estimate of the current configuration's reward, $\kappa(\cdot\mid x_0)^\top r(U_P)$, with its exact value $r(x_0)$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:density-readout}
\widehat Q_r(s,a) = \frac{ r(x_0) + \left( w-\kappa(\cdot\mid x_0) \right)^\top r(U_P) }{ 1-\gamma }.
\end{equation}
The correction is action independent, so it changes calibration but never action ranking.
The resulting readout is linear in $r$ and requires no retraining.
To act, we retrieve the pool observation that maximizes $\widehat Q_r$ from the initial state, condition the goal-reaching actor on it, and roll out.

\paragraph{Reward construction.}
We test three reward families, all defined only from object coordinates, valued in $[0,1]$, and never observed during training (Figure~\ref{fig:rewardfams}; internal names \texttt{dirac}/\texttt{dense}/\texttt{region}):
\emph{sparse}, the benchmark's tolerance indicator $\ind{\|x-g\|\le\epsilon}$ (conjunctive over object blocks) at the five task goals;
\emph{dense}, the same centers with distance-based shaping $\exp(-d(x,g)/2\epsilon)$ in tolerance-scaled coordinates;
and \emph{held-out regions}, the same sparse indicator centered at reachable dataset configurations that are not benchmark goals (drawn by hindsight from within episodes, kept only with at least five relabeled samples inside the region).
Dense versus sparse tests sensitivity to reward shaping while keeping the goals fixed; sparse versus held-out regions keeps the reward form fixed while changing where in object space the reward sits.
$25$ episodes per task, three seeds.
The fixed-partition row is the same objective with the continuous query set replaced by $256$ fixed prototypes, the discretized ablation.

This is consistent with continuous query representations helping most when the reward sits between or away from fixed prototypes.
The hard target outperforms the soft target on all three reward families while the two perform almost identically on goal reaching (Table~\ref{tab:main}), suggesting that kernel smoothing degrades reward transfer in this setting without helping control.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Successor representations and measures predict discounted future occupancy \citep{dayan1993improving,blier2021learning}; FB factorizes the density and retargets a policy family to downstream rewards \citep{touati2021learning,touati2023does}.
Goal-conditioned offline RL studies the same reach-a-goal problem with sparse rewards and hindsight relabeling \citep{kaelbling1993learning,andrychowicz2017hindsight,kostrikov2021offline,park2023hiql}; goal-conditioned IQL is our representative baseline from that family.
Contrastive RL \citep{eysenbach2022contrastive} learns future-state discrimination; \DensityFB{} keeps the FB factorization and an explicit normalized successor-measure interpretation.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The method is given the object coordinates that define goals; pixels would need a learned object representation.
Queries are the batch's own successors; independent query pools are untested.
Calibration against Monte-Carlo region masses is strong at coarse scale (Spearman $0.83$--$0.85$ at $4\epsilon$) and moderate at the native scale ($0.41$--$0.48$), for the partition variant as well, so fine-scale set mass is a limit of the estimator family; the hard variant's calibration is not yet measured.
On \texttt{scene}, both composed-rearrangement tasks fail across seeds and both variants, suggesting that structure beyond geometric object coordinates is needed there.
