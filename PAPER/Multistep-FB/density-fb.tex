\section{Setting}
\label{sec:prelim}

The successor measure of a policy $\pi$ is its discounted future-state occupancy,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sm}
M^\pi(s,a,X) = \E_\pi\!\left[ \sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^t \ind{s_{t+1}\in X} \;\middle|\; s_0=s,a_0=a \right],
\qquad
Q_r^\pi(s,a) = \int r(x)\,dM^\pi(s,a,x),
\end{equation}
so one learned measure evaluates any downstream reward \citep{dayan1993improving,blier2021learning}.
Forward-backward (FB) representations \citep{touati2021learning,touati2023does} factorize its density against the dataset marginal $\rho$, $m_z(s,a,x) = F(s,a,z)^\top B(x)$, embed a reward as $z_r \propto \E_{x\sim\rho}[r(x)B(x)]$, and read values as $Q_r \approx F(s,a,z_r)^\top z_r$.
The standard estimator is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fb-loss}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FB}} = \tfrac12 \E_{x\sim\rho} \left[ (m_z-\gamma\bar m_z)^2 \right] - \E \left[ m_z(s,a,s') - \gamma\bar m_z(s,a,s') \right] + \lambda_{\mathrm{ortho}} \mathcal L_{\mathrm{ortho}}:
\end{equation}
random queries get a squared residual; the observed successor gets a linear positive term.

\section{The issue: play data inflates the successor loss}
\label{sec:play}

Manipulation play alternates brief object-changing interactions with long periods at persistent configurations: approach, grasp, transport, release, then rest.
On \texttt{cube-single-play-v0} the cube is stationary on $48\%$ of transitions and stationary runs have median length $44$ steps; after entering a $4$\,cm region a trajectory accumulates $25$--$31$ discounted visits there.
On \texttt{cube-double} each cube is stationary on $72\%$ of transitions; on \texttt{scene}, drawer and window on $91\%$ and buttons on $99.6\%$ (Figure~\ref{fig:playdata}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{figures/playdata.png}
\caption{\emph{Left:} three \texttt{cube-single} play trajectories. \emph{Right:} discounted occupancy accumulated after first entering a $4$\,cm goal region. Most successor mass accumulates after arrival.}
\label{fig:playdata}
\end{figure}

This structure inflates the loss.
A rest of length $L$ contributes $L$ near-identical positive terms, one per transition, while the reaching decision that produced it contributed once; at the median $L=44$ the objective weights ``the cube stays here'' about forty times more heavily than ``this action brought it here.''
The linear observed-successor term in Equation~\ref{eq:fb-loss} turns this into unbounded scale growth, and the region-indicator form of the objective admits a large, well-scaled, action-independent constant $c^\star = \E_\rho[h]/(1-\gamma)$ that satisfies the Bellman equation on average.
Either way, a critic reaches a low loss without learning anything about actions.

The failure is the estimator, not the representation.
With a backward representation frozen from a checkpoint supporting $0.97$ task success, the scalar FB loss grows the critic scale from $154$ to $5.4\times10^4$ within $65$k steps and success falls to $0.42$; orthonormalization bounds the scale but caps success at $0.225$.

\section{\DensityFB: soft and hard variants}
\label{sec:method}

Since $M(\mathcal X\mid s,a) = 1/(1-\gamma)$ for every $(s,a)$, the normalized measure
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nu}
\nu(\cdot\mid s,a,g) = (1-\gamma)\, M(\cdot\mid s,a,g)
\end{equation}
is a probability distribution over object coordinates $x=\phi_{\mathrm{obj}}(s)$ carrying the same information as $M$.
We keep the FB two towers but use the dot product as a score rather than a density,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:density-softmax}
f(s,a,g,u) = F(s,a,g)^\top B(\tilde u)/\tau,
\qquad
w_j(s,a,g) = \frac{\exp f(s,a,g,u_j)}{\sum_k \exp f(s,a,g,u_k)},
\end{equation}
over a query set $U=\{u_j\}$ redrawn from the dataset marginal at every update (the batch's own next object configurations).
Non-negativity and unit mass hold by construction, and the query count is a Monte-Carlo sample size, not a discretization: the checkpoint stores only $F$ and $B$.

The target mixes an immediate distribution around the observed successor with the bootstrapped prediction, and is fit by cross-entropy:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:density-loss}
t = \sg \left[ (1-\gamma)\, \kappa(\cdot\mid x') + \gamma\, \bar p(s',a^+,g) \right],
\qquad
\mathcal L = - \E \Big[ \sum_j t_j \log w_j \Big],
\qquad a^+ \sim \pi_{\bar\omega}(\cdot\mid s',g).
\end{equation}
Both terms lie on the simplex, so $t$ is itself a distribution.
We evaluate two immediate targets:
\begin{align}
\text{soft:}\quad &\kappa_\epsilon(u_j\mid x') \propto \exp\!\left( -\tfrac12 \|(u_j-x')/\epsilon\|^2 \right),
\label{eq:kappa-soft}\\
\text{hard:}\quad &\kappa(u_j\mid x') = \ind{u_j = x'},
\label{eq:kappa-hard}
\end{align}
with $\epsilon$ the task tolerance; the hard target is well defined because $U$ contains each row's successor.

This removes the inflation without deleting dwell mass.
Rests can concentrate the predicted distribution but cannot grow the critic, because total mass is fixed; the action-independent solution becomes the uniform distribution, which cross-entropy penalizes against any concentrated target and which is visible in the prediction's entropy.
The immediate target sets how dwell is credited: hard credits the realized successor exactly, soft spreads the same mass over its $\epsilon$-neighborhood.

For control, the actor maximizes the predicted mass near its goal, $q(s,a,g) = w(s,a,g)^\top \kappa(\cdot\mid g)$, computed at frozen measure parameters.

\section{Goal-conditioned results}
\label{sec:main-results}

OGBench state-based manipulation \citep{park2024ogbench}, $500$k gradient steps, no reward labels during training, native evaluation (five tasks, $50$ episodes each), three seeds, mean $\pm$ $95\%$ confidence interval ($t$-based, $n{=}3$).

Plain FB fails on every environment and collapses entirely beyond one object, consistent with Section~\ref{sec:play}.

On \texttt{scene} both variants more than double goal-conditioned IQL, with the same per-task split on every seed: \texttt{open}, \texttt{unlock-and-lock} and \texttt{rearrange-medium} are solved ($0.88$--$1.0$) while \texttt{put-in-drawer} ($0.02$--$0.50$) and \texttt{rearrange-hard} ($0.0$--$0.04$) fail.

\section{Reward evaluation}
\label{sec:reward-transfer}

The point of a successor measure is that one model evaluates any reward.
For a test reward $r$ we draw a pool $U_P$ of $16{,}384$ object configurations from the dataset, evaluate $r$ on the pool, and read
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:density-readout}
\widehat Q_r(s,a) = \frac{ r(x_0) + \left( w-\kappa(\cdot\mid x_0) \right)^\top r(U_P) }{ 1-\gamma },
\end{equation}
which is exactly linear in $r$ and needs no retraining.
To act, we retrieve the pool observation that maximizes $\widehat Q_r$ from the initial state, condition the goal-reaching actor on it, and roll out.
Three held-out reward families, $25$ episodes per task, three seeds: point goals (dirac), shaped point goals (dense), and extended regions.
The fixed-partition row is the same objective with the continuous query set replaced by $256$ fixed prototypes, the discretized ablation.

The hard target improves every family while leaving goal reaching unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:main}), so the soft kernel's smoothing was costing transfer rather than protecting it.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Successor representations and measures predict discounted future occupancy \citep{dayan1993improving,blier2021learning}; FB factorizes the density and retargets a policy family to downstream rewards \citep{touati2021learning,touati2023does}.
Goal-conditioned offline RL studies the same reach-a-goal problem with sparse rewards and hindsight relabeling \citep{kaelbling1993learning,andrychowicz2017hindsight,kostrikov2021offline,park2023hiql}; goal-conditioned IQL is our representative baseline from that family.
Contrastive RL \citep{eysenbach2022contrastive} learns future-state discrimination; \DensityFB{} keeps the FB factorization and an explicit normalized successor-measure interpretation.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The method is given the object coordinates that define goals; pixels would need a learned object representation.
Queries are the batch's own successors; independent query pools are untested.
Calibration against Monte-Carlo region masses is strong at coarse scale (Spearman $0.83$--$0.85$ at $4\epsilon$) and moderate at the native scale ($0.41$--$0.48$), for the partition variant as well, so fine-scale set mass is a limit of the estimator family; the hard variant's calibration is not yet measured.
On \texttt{scene}, both composed-rearrangement tasks fail across seeds and both variants, suggesting that structure beyond geometric object coordinates is needed there.
