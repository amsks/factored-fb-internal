\section{Introduction}

Unsupervised reinforcement learning (URL) promises reusable control: an agent learns a policy, representation, or value structure without task rewards and later adapts it to downstream goals.
This is especially appealing in robotics, where the same workspace may contain many possible goals, but collecting new interaction data for every reward is expensive.
Many URL objectives instantiate this idea by learning some form of future-reachability object, including goal-conditioned values, contrastive state-goal scores, successor measures, or predictive representations~\citep{eysenbach-iclr21a,eysenbach-neurips22a,ma-neurips22a,touati-neurips21a,touati-iclr23a,agarwal-rlj25a}.

Despite substantial successes in locomotion and navigation style domains~\citep{touati-iclr23a,trinzoni-iclr25a,agarwal-arxiv25a}, manipulation remains a persistent class of challenging problems for URL methods~\citep{}. 
In this work, we identify that the non-smooth dynamics of manipulation illustrate a fundamental challenge for smooth, low-rank approximators used in URL methods. In particular:
\begin{hypothesis}[Interaction-conditioned successor gap hypothesis]
State-based URL methods can fail when observation-near states belong to different interaction regimes with different long-horizon successor measures.
In cube manipulation, this means that a policy may solve coarse reachability and acquire control of the object, while still failing to preserve the controlled-object regime and transport the cube to the goal.
\end{hypothesis}
We formalize this as a local successor gap: a sharp change in a task-relevant successor functional between observation-near states.
% We formalize the notion of non-smooth dynamics, and illustrate how inner-product spaces can struggle to capture this property, which may explain poor performance. 

We use cube manipulation as a diagnostic instantiation of this hypothesis.
The phase structure is physically interpretable: approach tests coarse reachability, control acquisition tests entry into the relevant interaction regime, maintained control tests persistence of that regime, and transport tests whether the policy exploits it to reach the downstream goal.
Our experiments show that failures are dominated by post-acquisition outcomes, and that learned scores weakly separate observation-near states with different futures.
This suggests that the failure is not explained by state-support coverage or coarse cube geometry alone.

We make three contributions.
\begin{inparaenum}[(i)]
    \item we formalize interaction-conditioned successor gaps: local interaction variables can induce sharp changes in task-relevant successor measures even between observation-near states, and observation-smooth scores incur error near such gaps; 
    \item we instantiate this phenomenon in cube pick-and-place through a phase diagnostic that decomposes failures into approach, control acquisition, maintained control, and transport; and 
    \item we apply the diagnostic to four state-based URL methods on \ogbench{} \task{}. 
\end{inparaenum}
The resulting failure profiles show that the main attrition occurs after control acquisition; support and value-ranking probes further suggest that neither state-support coverage nor coarse cube geometry fully explains the failure pattern.
