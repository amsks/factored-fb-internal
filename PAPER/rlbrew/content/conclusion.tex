\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied where state-based unsupervised RL methods fail in contact-rich cube manipulation.
Rather than treating success as a single binary outcome, we decomposed each rollout into approach, grasp, maintained control, and transport.
Across four URL methods, aggregate success hides a consistent failure profile: policies rarely fail to reach the cube, and often acquire control, but many failures occur after acquisition, when the cube must remain controllable and be transported to the goal.
Support diagnostics show that contact-regime coverage contributes to this failure mode, but does not fully explain the method ordering.
Value-ranking diagnostics further suggest that learned scores only weakly distinguish observation-near states with different post-contact futures.

These results point to post-contact controllability as a useful target for future offline URL objectives in manipulation.
The goal is not only to learn representations that encode where the robot, object, and goal are, but also to preserve the interaction-conditioned distinctions that determine whether control of the object can be maintained.
We view the phase diagnostic as a simple tool for making these failures visible: it separates coarse reachability, control acquisition, maintained control, and transport, and therefore turns a scalar success rate into a more actionable failure profile.
