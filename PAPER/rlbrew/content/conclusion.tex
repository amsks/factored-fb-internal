\section{Conclusion}

We studied where state-based future-reachability methods fail in contact-rich cube manipulation.
Our first step was to decompose each rollout into approach, control acquisition, maintained control, and transport.
Across five methods, aggregate success hides a consistent failure profile: policies rarely fail to reach the cube and often acquire coarse control, but many failures occur \emph{after} acquisition, when the post-interaction tail must preserve control and reach the goal.
Support diagnostics show that state-space coverage covaries with performance but does not fully explain it: sampling initial grasp states from the offline dataset shows that initial-state support alone is not a complete explanation, though it does not rule out limited action or transition support during the subsequent continuation. On these in-dataset states, \rldp{} and \psm{} still rank outcomes near chance and \fb{} only weakly, while \crl{} is intermediate.
Value-ranking diagnostics further suggest that the weakest methods' learned scores (\rldp{}, \psm{}, and to a lesser extent \fb{}) only weakly distinguish
in-hand post-acquisition states with different post-interaction futures.
These results point to interaction-conditioned successor structure as a useful target for future offline goal-conditioned and reward-free control methods.
The goal is not only to encode where the robot, object, and goal are, but also to preserve the interaction-mode distinctions that determine which tail successor regime follows a local interaction event.
