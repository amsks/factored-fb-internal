\section{Interaction-conditioned successor gaps}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Successor measures under local interaction modes}

Successor measures summarize long-horizon future occupancy under a policy.
Their usefulness depends on whether the learned representation preserves the distinctions that change future occupancy.
In many control problems, these distinctions are not purely geometric.
A local interaction variable can change how actions affect the environment, splitting nearby observations into different effective dynamics.
Local interaction variables have long been studied in structured MDPs and contact-rich control~\citep{boutilier-ijcai95a,kearns-ijcai99a,guestrin-jair03a,mason-ijrr86a,lynch-ijrr96a,posa-ijrr14a,pfrommer-corl20a}.


Let $\tilde{s}\in\widetilde{\mathcal S}$ denote the full physical state and let $y=\Omega(\tilde{s})\in\mathcal Y$ denote the observation used by the policy or value function.
We write $\iota(\tilde{s})\in\mathcal I$ for an interaction mode.
The mode may encode contact state, object attachment, latch state, foot support phase, possession of an object, or any other local variable that changes how actions couple to future state.
We write the full-state transition kernel as interaction-conditioned:
\[
     P(d\tilde{s}'\mid \tilde{s},a) = P_{\iota(\tilde{s})}(d\tilde{s}'\mid \Omega(\tilde{s}),a).
\]
This notation emphasizes that two full states can have nearby observations but different effective transition kernels because their interaction modes differ.

For a task indexed by $g$, let $\varphi_g(\tilde{s}_{0:H})\in[0,1]$ denote a finite-horizon success functional.
It may represent ordinary goal reaching, a reach-avoid event, or a task-specific condition such as reaching a goal while preserving control.
We define the task-relevant successor functional
\[
    u^\pi_H(\tilde{s},a;g) = \mathbb{E}_\pi \left[ \varphi_g(\tilde{s}_{0:H}) \mid \tilde{s}_0=\tilde{s},a_0=a  \right]
\]
This is a scalar projection of the successor measure summarizing whether the future occupancy induced by $(\tilde{s},a)$ satisfies the downstream event.

\subsection{Local successor gaps}
For an observation scale $\epsilon>0$, define the local interaction-conditioned successor gap
\[
    \Delta^\pi_{\epsilon,H}(g) = \sup_{\substack{\tilde{s}_1,\tilde{s}_2,a\\ \|\Omega(\tilde{s}_1)-\Omega(\tilde{s}_2)\|\leq\epsilon}}  \left | u^\pi_H(\tilde{s}_1,a;g) - u^\pi_H(\tilde{s}_2,a;g) \right |
\]
A large value of $\Delta^\pi_{\epsilon,H}(g)$ means that observation-near states have different long-horizon futures.
This can occur even when the observations are geometrically close, because the interaction mode changes the transition kernel and therefore the successor measure.
For example, a grasped object and a slipping object may have nearby poses but different transport futures.
Similarly, a latched and unlatched door may look similar but induce different reachable regions.
In each case, the relevant successor object is conditioned on a local interaction variable that may be weakly represented or smoothed over by a state-based score.
Such mode-dependent dynamics are common in contact-rich manipulation and hybrid control~\citep{mason-ijrr86a,lynch-ijrr96a,posa-ijrr14a,manchester-isrr17a,pfrommer-corl20a}.

Ideally, a full-state successor measure should be able to represent the interaction mode.
The issue is approximation after observation compression: if the learned score is smooth in an observation geometry that does not separate interaction regimes, then it must make error near local successor gaps.

\begin{proposition}[Observation-smooth scores incur error at local successor gaps]
Let $h_\theta(y,a;g)$ be an observation-based approximation to $u^\pi_H(\tilde{s},a;g)$.
Assume that $h_\theta$ is $L$-Lipschitz in $y$ for fixed $a$ and $g$:
\[
    |h_\theta(y_1,a;g)-h_\theta(y_2,a;g)| \leq L\|y_1-y_2\|.
\]
If there exist $\tilde{s}_1,\tilde{s}_2$ and $a$ such that \(\|\Omega(\tilde{s}_1)-\Omega(\tilde{s}_2)\|\leq\epsilon\) and 
\[
    \left| u^\pi_H(\tilde{s}_1,a;g) - u^\pi_H(\tilde{s}_2,a;g) \right| = \Delta,
\]
then
\[
    \max_{i\in\{1,2\}} \left| h_\theta(\Omega(\tilde{s}_i),a;g) - u^\pi_H(\tilde{s}_i,a;g) \right| \geq \frac{(\Delta-L\epsilon)_+}{2},
\]
\end{proposition}

The proof is a direct triangle-inequality argument and appears in Appendix~\ref{app:proof_interaction_gap}.
The proposition says that if the true task-relevant successor functional changes by $\Delta$ inside an $\epsilon$-ball, then an $L$-Lipschitz observation-based score can explain at most $L\epsilon$ of that change.
The remaining discrepancy must appear as approximation error on at least one of the two states.

\subsection{Implication for learned URL scores}

Many URL methods induce a scalar goal-conditioned score $h_\theta(y,a;g)$.
\gciql{} learns such a score as a neural $Q(y,a,g)$.
\crl{} and \fb{} instantiate it through inner products.
\rldp{} induces such a score through rollout or extraction from a predictive representation.
The proposition applies whenever the induced score is smooth in the observation and does not separately preserve interaction mode.

The inner-product case makes the constraint explicit.
If
\[
    h_\theta(y,a;g)=f_\theta(y,a)^\top b_\theta(g),
\]
with $\|b_\theta(g)\|_2\leq B$ and
\[
    \|f_\theta(y_1,a)-f_\theta(y_2,a)\|_2 \leq L_f\|y_1-y_2\|,
\]
then
\begin{equation}
|h_\theta(y_1,a;g)-h_\theta(y_2,a;g)| \leq B L_f \|y_1-y_2\|.
\end{equation}
Thus the lower bound becomes
\[ 
    \max_{i\in\{1,2\}} \left| h_\theta(\Omega(\tilde{s}_i),a;g) - u^\pi_H(\tilde{s}_i,a;g) \right| \geq \frac{(\Delta-BL_f\epsilon)_+}{2}.
\]
This identifies the pressure placed on a single global score geometry: it must represent smooth geometric proximity and sharp interaction-conditioned changes in future occupancy at the same time.
If the score smooths across interaction regimes, it may underestimate states from which the task remains controllable or overestimate nearby states whose future controllability has already been lost.

\subsection{Cube manipulation as an instantiation}

We instantiate this general phenomenon in \ogbench{} \task{}.
Here, the interaction mode corresponds to whether the cube is freely moving, controllably held, slipping, or dropped.
The observation contains gripper pose, cube pose, gripper opening, and contact indicators, but it does not fully specify contact quality, frictional margin, or incipient slip.
Thus two observations can be close in cube pose and gripper pose while inducing different transport futures.

For goal $g$, we use the task-specific event
\[
    \varphi_g(\tilde{s}_{0:H}) = \mathbf{1} \{
        \tau_A\leq H,\;
        \tau_C\leq H,\;
        \tilde{s}_H\in M,\;
        \tilde{s}_H\in G_g
    \},
\]
where $A$ is the approach set, $C$ is the control-acquired set, $M$ is the final maintained-control set, and $G_g$ is the goal set.
The resulting scalar functional is
\begin{equation}
    u^\pi_H(\tilde{s},a;g) = \Pr_\pi \left(
        \tau_A\leq H,\;
        \tau_C\leq H,\;
        \tilde{s}_H\in M,\;
        \tilde{s}_H\in G_g
        \mid
        \tilde{s}_0=\tilde{s},a_0=a
    \right).
\end{equation}
The diagnostic in the next section decomposes failures of this event into the first unmet precondition.
In this domain, approach tests coarse reachability, control acquisition tests entry into the relevant interaction regime, maintained control tests persistence of that regime, and transport tests whether the policy exploits the regime to reach the downstream goal.
