\begin{abstract}
Unsupervised and goal-conditioned reinforcement learning methods often learn reusable future-reachability objects, such as goal-conditioned values, contrastive scores, successor measures, or predictive representations.
We study a failure mode that arises when the observation or learned representation under-resolves local interaction variables.
In the full physical state, the successor measure is well defined; the difficulty is that observation-near states can belong to different interaction regimes with different post-interaction futures.
A learned score may therefore preserve coarse geometric reachability while weakly distinguishing the interaction variable that determines future controllability.
We formalize this view through interaction-conditioned tail successor structure and instantiate it in the state-based version of \ogbench{} \task{}, where the relevant interaction variable is whether the cube is freely moving, controllably held, slipping, or dropped.
A phase diagnostic decomposes each rollout into approach, control acquisition, maintained control, and transport.
Across four state-based future-reachability methods, approach failures are negligible, while most failures occur after control acquisition.
Support diagnostics show that contact-regime coverage contributes but does not determine the method ordering, and value-ranking probes show that learned scores weakly separate states corresponding to cube in-hand with different post-interaction futures.
These results suggest that the missing object is not grasping-specific, but the preservation of interaction-conditioned successor structure.
\end{abstract}
