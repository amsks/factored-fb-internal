\begin{abstract}
Unsupervised reinforcement learning (URL) aims to learn reusable representations, value structures, or policy families that can be adapted to downstream rewards without task-specific pretraining. 
Contact-rich manipulation is a challenging setting for this paradigm because future reachability is not determined by geometry alone: moving to a cube, acquiring contact, maintaining a grasp, and transporting the object correspond to different interaction regimes. 
In such tasks, nearby observed states can have sharply different long-horizon futures when they differ in contact stability. 
This paper investigates whether failures of state-based URL methods in cube manipulation can be explained by these interaction-conditioned successor gaps. 
We formalize manipulation through a reach-before-drop successor quantity and show how observation-smooth successor approximations can incur error near contact-induced discontinuities. 
We then introduce a rollout diagnostic that assigns each failure to the first unmet physical precondition: approach, grasp, maintain grasp, or transport. 
On \ogbench{} \task{}, aggregate success obscures the relevant failure mode. \gciql{} achieves higher overall success than \fb{}, but \fb{} reaches the cube in all analyzed rollouts and has fewer initial grasp failures; its deficit is concentrated after pick-up, through grasp-maintenance and transport failures. Extending the decomposition across state-based URL methods shows that the central difficulty is not simply reaching the object or acquiring initial contact, but preserving interaction-conditioned controllability after contact.
\end{abstract}
