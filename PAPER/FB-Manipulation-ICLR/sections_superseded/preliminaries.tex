\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

\paragraph{Successor measures.}
For a policy $\pi$ in a discounted MDP, the successor measure is the discounted
occupancy of state sets,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sm}
M^{\pi}(s,a,X) \;=\; \E_{\pi}\Big[\textstyle\sum_{t\ge 0}\gamma^{t}\,
\ind{s_{t+1}\in X}\ \Big|\ s_{0}=s,\,a_{0}=a\Big],
\end{equation}
a measure over $X$ for each $(s,a)$ \citep{dayan1993improving,blier2021learning}.
Its appeal is that it turns reward specification into integration: for any reward
$r$, the action-value of $\pi$ is $Q_r(s,a)=\int r\,dM^{\pi}(s,a,\cdot)$. A single
trained object therefore serves every reward, which is the premise of zero-shot
reinforcement learning \citep{touati2021learning,touati2023does}.

\paragraph{The forward--backward factorization.}
Forward--backward (FB) representations make this tractable by factorizing the
measure with respect to a reference distribution $\rho$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fb}
M^{\pi_z}(s,a,X) \;\approx\; \int_X F(s,a,z)^{\top}B(x)\,d\rho(x),
\end{equation}
where $z$ indexes the \emph{continuation policy} $\pi_z$ and $x$ is the
\emph{queried future state}. The two arguments are independent: one fixed policy,
the occupancy of every query state under it. A reward specified by labeled samples
is consumed through the moment readout $z_r \propto \E[r(x)B(x)]$, after which
$\langle F(s,a,z_r), z_r\rangle$ is that reward's action-value and $\pi_{z_r}$ is a
policy for it, with no further training.

\paragraph{Setting.}
We work on OGBench \texttt{cube-single-play-v0} \citep{park2024ogbench}: a
six-DoF arm and one cube, $28$-dimensional state observations, and roughly $10^{6}$
offline transitions of unlabeled ``play'' in which a scripted policy repeatedly
picks the cube up, moves it, and sets it down. No reward labels are used at
training time. This domain is where future-reachability methods visibly fail:
FB variants plateau near $0.42$ overall success while goal-conditioned IQL
\citep{kostrikov2021offline} with an identical bilinear critic and the same actor
class reaches $0.93$ on the same data. Since everything is offline and shared, the
gap is not exploration or coverage; something about the measure \emph{objective}
fails here.

\paragraph{Assumption: a given region structure.}
Throughout we assume access to a state abstraction $\cube(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$ ---
the cube position --- and a radius $\epsilon$, and we take the queried sets to be
balls in that abstraction,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:region}
X = B_\epsilon(g) \;=\; \{x : \|\cube(x)-\cube(g)\| \le \epsilon\},
\qquad \epsilon = 4\,\text{cm}.
\end{equation}
We state this plainly because it is a real assumption and it is doing real work.
Choosing $\cube$ and $\epsilon$ is choosing which states count as the same
outcome, and in unsupervised RL that choice is normally what a representation is
supposed to discover. Ours is supplied: $\cube$ selects three of twenty-eight
observation dimensions, and $\epsilon$ coincides with the threshold the benchmark
uses to declare success. What follows is therefore a method for learning a
successor measure \emph{over a given region structure}, not for discovering the
structure. Section~\ref{sec:limitations} states what is and is not known about
how much this assumption buys.

\paragraph{What we train: the diagonal slice.}
Equation~\ref{eq:fb} carries two free arguments, but the object we train through
most of this paper is the diagonal slice
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:diag}
g \;\longmapsto\; M^{\pi_g}\!\big(s,a,B_\epsilon(g)\big),
\end{equation}
the occupancy of a goal region \emph{under that goal's own continuation policy},
obtained by tying $z=\psi(g)$ to $x=g$. The distinction matters and is classical
\citep{blier2021learning}: summing the slice over two rewarded goals combines
quantities computed under two \emph{different} policies, so the sum is not the
value of any single policy. The slice suffices for goal reaching and for ranking a
small candidate set, and it is where the training-stability content of the method
lives. Section~\ref{sec:exp-l2} evaluates the separated form, in which $z$ and $x$
are trained as independent arguments and an arbitrary reward's $z_r$ is consumed
directly.

\paragraph{Notation.}
We write $h(s',g)=\ind{\|\cube(s')-\cube(g)\|\le\epsilon}\in\{0,1\}$ for the hit
indicator of a region, $\bar{\,\cdot\,}$ for a stop-gradient target-network value,
and $\hat N(g)=\rho(B_\epsilon(g))$ for the data mass of a region. Batches supply
an $n\times n$ matrix of residuals: every state in the batch is a query state for
every other, so $h$ is an $n\times n$ binary matrix whose diagonal is $1$.
