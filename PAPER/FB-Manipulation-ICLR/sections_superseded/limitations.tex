\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{The region structure is given, and we do not know how much it buys.}
Section~\ref{sec:prelim} states the assumption; here is its cost. The abstraction
$\cube$ selects three of twenty-eight observation dimensions, and the radius
$\epsilon$ coincides with the threshold the benchmark uses to score success, so
both the variable and the scale of the goal regions are supplied from the
evaluation. We have one measurement bearing on this: a control that keeps FB's
original loss and generalizes only the support from a Dirac to our ball reaches
$0.367$, far short of the $0.93$ the bounded objective attains, which shows the
region alone is not sufficient. The converse experiment --- the bounded objective
on an \emph{unprivileged} support, whether a Dirac, a learned feature, or a slice
unrelated to the evaluation variable --- has not been run, so we cannot say the
objective is a general recipe rather than one that works when handed the right
abstraction. This is the single most important open experiment in the paper.

Relatedly, the $48$ transfer rewards of Section~\ref{sec:exp-l2} are novel reward
\emph{functions} over that same given abstraction. They are not novel
abstractions, and ``zero-shot'' should be read in that narrower sense.

\paragraph{The object is not non-negative, and no repair we found conserves mass.}
Forty-five percent of the state--goal product carries negative measure. The
one-sided hinge removes the sign violation but inflates set mass by
$2.5$--$3.6\times$; the task-vector fix of Section~\ref{sec:exp-rewardz} leaves
negativity untouched at $0.43$--$0.47$. Sign and scale have traded off against
each other in every intervention we have tried, and a parameterization that
enforces non-negativity while preserving both the total mass and the linearity in
$\psi$ that the $\langle F,z_r\rangle$ readout requires remains open.

\paragraph{All measure-side evidence is one environment.}
Calibration, transfer, and the task-vector experiment are \texttt{cube-single}
only. Section~\ref{sec:exp-suite} shows the visitation backup --- the one that
yields a measure at all --- \emph{loses} $0.19$ and $0.14$ on the two harder
environments, so the capability is currently demonstrated only where it happens to
be free. Whether the measure results survive where the backup costs something is
untested, and it is the obvious place for the story to break.

\paragraph{No competing method has been run through the transfer harness.}
The numbers in Tables~\ref{tab:l2} and \ref{tab:rewardz} compare our method
against its own oracle and its own floor. We have not run the goal-conditioned
anchor, vanilla FB, or any other successor-measure method through the same $48$
rewards, so we can report that the method beats an uninformative baseline but not
that it beats an alternative. Two further baseline gaps: the floor is a single
draw of $z$ shared across all $48$ rewards, and the ceiling is a proxy for the
optimum that understates performance on precisely the broad families that score
worst.

\paragraph{Statistical power.}
Cells carry 2--3 seeds. Two readings in particular should be treated as
suggestive: the task-success \emph{improvement} in Section~\ref{sec:exp-rewardz}
($0.887\to0.95$, about two seed standard deviations on two seeds), and any
per-family ordering among arms in Table~\ref{tab:rewardz}. The primary
broad-reward effect is larger and consistent across both seeds and all three arms,
which is why we report it as a result rather than a trend.

\paragraph{The calibrated numbers depend on a learned density head.}
Table~\ref{tab:calib}'s headline ratio divides by a trained $\hat N$ whose own
bias is $+28\%$, in the direction that flatters the ratio. With exact counts the
same object is $1.9$--$3.2\times$ off. We report both columns for this reason, and
the honest statement is that the \emph{deployed system} is calibrated while
$M$ itself is roughly a factor of two high.

\paragraph{Conclusions are about the objective, not the actor.}
Every cell uses one actor class (flow BC with one-step distillation). An
actor-isolation sweep on a different actor showed the advantage over the anchor
reversing at one setting, on a single seed, and we have not resolved it. Claims
here should be read as claims about the value objective.

\paragraph{Scope of the theory.}
The contraction argument behind Equation~\ref{eq:backups} applies to an idealized
fixed-policy evaluation operator. The practical estimator --- a changing actor, a
rank-$50$ bilinear approximation, twin-min backups, offline bootstrap actions, and
a $\psi$ trained for a different target --- carries no such guarantee. Stability
here is an empirical finding, not a theorem.
