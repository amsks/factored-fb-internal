\section{Bounded region regression}
\label{sec:method}

This section isolates a structural defect in how successor measures are trained,
and shows that a single substitution removes it. The argument is self-contained:
it needs no assumption about the geometry of the data and no factorization of the
measure. It also incurs a debt, made explicit in Section~\ref{sec:owed}, which
Section~\ref{sec:exp-scale} later collects.

\subsection{The immediate term is singular}
\label{sec:singular}

Let $m_z(s,a,x)=dM^{\pi_z}(s,a,\cdot)/d\rho\,(x)$ be the density of the successor
measure with respect to the reference $\rho$ --- the object FB factorizes as
$F(s,a,z)^{\top}B(x)$. It satisfies the density Bellman equation
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:density-bellman}
m_z(s,a,x)
\;=\;
\underbrace{\frac{\delta_{s'}(x)}{\rho(x)}}_{\text{immediate}}
\;+\;
\gamma\,\E_{a'\sim\pi_z(\cdot\mid s')}\big[m_z(s',a',x)\big].
\end{equation}
The immediate term is a Dirac density ratio. On the observed successor it is a
point mass divided by a density, not a finite scalar, so it cannot be placed
inside a squared Bellman residual: there is no target to regress toward. FB
therefore does not regress Equation~\ref{eq:density-bellman} at all. It minimizes
a least-squares density-matching surrogate,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fb-loss}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{FB}}
=\tfrac12\,\E_{x\sim\rho}\big[(m_z-\gamma \bar m_z)^2\big]
\;-\;\E\big[m_z(s,a,s')-\gamma \bar m_z(s,a,s')\big]
\;+\;\lambda_{\mathrm{ortho}}\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ortho}},
\end{equation}
in which random queries $x\sim\rho$ carry a quadratic residual while the observed
successor enters only through a \emph{negative linear} term.

That asymmetry is the defect. The linear term has no fixed target. It pays
unconditionally for making the matched-successor value larger, without bound, and
the only thing holding its scale is the orthonormality regularizer on $B$. The two
standard stabilizers of FB are thus load-bearing in opposite directions:
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ortho}}$ anchors the scale, but it also imposes a geometry on
$B$ that the reaching structure of the task does not want. Removing it lets the
diagonal diverge; keeping it damages control. Section~\ref{sec:exp-diverge}
measures both halves of this trade directly, on a frozen embedding known to
support good control, so that neither outcome can be attributed to representation
quality.

\subsection{One substitution}
\label{sec:substitution}

Replace the point query by a finite region. With the hit indicator
$h(s',g)=\ind{\|\cube(s')-\cube(g)\|\le\epsilon}$ of Equation~\ref{eq:region}, the
singular immediate term becomes a bounded scalar:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:substitution}
\frac{\delta_{s'}(x)}{\rho(x)}
\;\;\longrightarrow\;\;
h(s',g) \in \{0,1\}.
\end{equation}
This is the entire change, and everything else follows from it. Every entry of the
$n\times n$ training matrix --- diagonal included --- now has an explicit,
representable target and is an ordinary squared residual. There is no separate
linear diagonal term, no off-diagonal mask, and no unmatched scale: the $0/1$
event pins the product $F^{\top}B$ directly, so no orthonormality regularizer, or
any regularizer, on $B$ is required. In one line,
\[
\text{density-ratio estimation}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\text{Bellman regression on finite regions}.
\]

\subsection{Two backups}
\label{sec:backups}

With a representable target, the choice of backup becomes a modeling decision
rather than a stability workaround. Two are natural, differing only in whether
arrival absorbs:
\begin{align}
\label{eq:backups}
y^{\mathrm{reach}}_{ij} &= h_{ij} + \gamma\,(1-h_{ij})\,\bar Q_{ij},
&& Q \in [0,1], \\
y^{\mathrm{visit}}_{ij} &= h_{ij} + \gamma\,\bar M_{ij},
&& M \in [0,\,1/(1-\gamma)].
\end{align}
The first terminates the backup at first arrival and learns a \emph{reaching}
value, $Q=\E[\gamma^{T_g}]$ for the hitting time $T_g$ of the region --- a
probability-like quantity that saturates on arrival and carries no revisit mass.
The second drops the $(1-h_{ij})$ factor so revisits keep accumulating, and learns
the \emph{visitation} measure of Equation~\ref{eq:sm}. Both losses are
$\E[(M_{ij}-y_{ij})^2]$ over the full matrix, optionally divided by
$\sg[\E[M^2]]$ to make the residual scale-free.

We train them as a two-phase curriculum: phase 1 with $y^{\mathrm{reach}}$, then
phase 2 with $y^{\mathrm{visit}}$ warm-started from it, with the backward map
$\psi$ frozen at the phase-1 geometry so that any phase-2 misbehavior is isolated
to the backup. The curriculum is an empirical choice, not a requirement --- in a
tabular setting where both can be solved exactly, absorbing-first and direct
visitation regression perform alike, which locates the curriculum's value in the
control loop rather than in policy evaluation.\footnote{A natural alternative motivation is the renewal factorization
$M = Q\cdot R$, occupancy as reaching times recurrence. It is exact for singleton
goals but only approximate for regions, and it fails pointwise on this data: the
recurrence factor retains an intraclass correlation of $0.24$--$0.29$ across
actions given a goal, against a pre-registered bar of $0.9$. We therefore do not
rest the method on it, and nothing in
Sections~\ref{sec:singular}--\ref{sec:backups} requires it.}

\subsection{What the region form owes}
\label{sec:owed}

The substitution buys boundedness, and it charges for it. The learned object is
the measure of a \emph{region}, $M(s,a,B_\epsilon(g))$, and a region's mass is not
a density. Two consequences follow, and both are predictions rather than
observations at this point in the argument.

First, integrating the learned object over goal centers double-counts: overlapping
balls share states, and the counting inherits whatever density the dataset places
over $g$. Writing $Q_r=\int r\,dM$ for the learned object is therefore not
justified as stated. The correction is a division by the region's own data mass,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nhat}
\int r\,dM \;\approx\; \sum_i r(x_i)\,\frac{M(s,a,B_\epsilon(x_i))}{\hat N(x_i)},
\qquad \hat N(g)=\rho(B_\epsilon(g)),
\end{equation}
so a readout that omits $1/\hat N$ should be wrong by a factor equal to the mean
region mass $\bar N = \E_g[\hat N(g)]$. This is a sharp, falsifiable prediction:
not merely that the scale is off, but by how much. Section~\ref{sec:exp-scale}
tests it.

Second, boundedness of the \emph{fixed point} does not imply non-negativity of the
\emph{estimate}. The target in Equation~\ref{eq:backups} is non-negative, but the
bilinear form $F^{\top}\psi$ is free to take negative values wherever the
regression is not pinned by data, and nothing in the loss penalizes it for doing
so. Section~\ref{sec:exp-calib} measures how often this happens, and finds the
obvious repair is not free.
