\section{Adding FB on top of IQL}
\label{sec:rgfb}

We start from GCIQL, which already works on Cube, and add the FB components (the bilinear task encoder, reward-embedding inference, and latent-conditioned policy) one piece at a time.
Each step changes exactly one thing.
Although the result retains FB's reward-embedding and latent-policy interface, it does not optimize the FB successor-measure objective (\texttt{measure\_coef}$=0$) and the forward representation $\phi$ is not conditioned on $z$: it is an FB-style zero-shot interface trained by GC-IQL rather than an FB representation.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Start from Bilinear-GCIQL.}
    A goal-conditioned critic $Q(s,a,g)$ and value $V(s,g)$, trained on hindsight-relabeled sparse reward $r\in\{-1,0\}$ (reward $0$ and mask $0$ at the goal) with the GC-IQL objective, expectile value regression and scalar Bellman regression:
    \begin{align}
        & \mathcal L_V = \E\big[\,\ell^\tau_2\big(\sg[Q(s,a,g)]-V(s,g)\big)\big], \\
        & \ell^\tau_2(u)=\big|\tau-\mathbf 1[u<0]\big|\,u^2, \\
        & \mathcal L_Q = \E\big[\big(r+\gamma\,(1{-}\mathrm{done})\,V(s',g)-Q(s,a,g)\big)^2\big]. \\
        & Q(s,a,g)=\frac{\phi(s,a)^\top\psi(g)}{\sqrt d}.
    \end{align}
    The expectile $\tau$ emphasizes high-value dataset-supported actions, so $V$ is pulled toward high-value continuations under the dataset action distribution.
    Train $\phi$ and $\psi$ jointly with $\mathcal L_V,\mathcal L_Q$.

    \item \textbf{Use the reward embedding.}
    Replace direct goal conditioning in the policy with the latent embedding $z=\psi(g)$: condition the flow policy on $\sg[z]$ rather than on $g$ (the critic is still trained on $g$).
    The policy now reaches the critic through a single latent $z\in\R^d$ (the latent task-conditioning interface).

    \item \textbf{Zero-shot reward inference.}
    For a new reward $r$ we never retrain.
    We estimate the reward moment $z_{\mathrm{raw}}$ by Monte Carlo sampling from the offline dataset, rescale it to the typical embedding norm to obtain $z_r$, and evaluate the same head:
    \begin{align}
        z_{\mathrm{raw}} &= \E_\rho[r(s')\,\psi(s')], &
        z_r &= c_r\,z_{\mathrm{raw}},\quad c_r = \overline{\|\psi(s')\|}\big/\|z_{\mathrm{raw}}\|, \\
        Q(s,a,z_{\mathrm{raw}}) &= \E_{s'\sim\rho}\!\Big[r(s')\,\tfrac{\phi(s,a)^\top\psi(s')}{\sqrt d}\Big], &
        Q(s,a,z_r) &= c_r\,Q(s,a,z_{\mathrm{raw}}).
    \end{align}
    By bilinearity, $Q(s,a,z_{\mathrm{raw}})$ is the reward-weighted linear combination of single-goal values (each $\phi(s,a)^\top\psi(s')/\sqrt d\approx Q(s,a,g{=}s')$), analogous to the successor-feature reward-composition identity and exact when the value is linear in the reward representation; the policy uses the norm-matched $z_r$, which rescales this value by $c_r$.
    The resulting policy is then evaluated as $\pi(\cdot\mid s,z_r)$.
    We use ``zero-shot'' to mean that no policy parameters are updated and no new environment interaction is required; the reward is evaluated on a fixed sample of offline states to construct $z_r$.
\end{enumerate}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:rgfb} gives the training step and the zero-shot reward inference: GC-IQL supervision in Phase 2, the latent task-conditioning interface in Phase 3, and the zero-shot evaluation in Phase 4.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Bellman-supervised FB-style interface}
\label{alg:rgfb}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]

\Require value features $\phi$, task encoder $\psi$ (=B), value features $\phi_V$, flow field $v_\theta$, direct actor $\pi_\eta$, and target networks $\bar\phi,\bar\psi$

\Statex \textbf{Phase 1: Transition \& Target Setup}
\State Sample transition $(s,a,s')$, hindsight goal $g$, and actor goal $g_\pi$
\State Construct sparse reward $r\in\{-1,0\}$ and continuation mask $m$
\State $\bar q\gets\min_k\bar\phi_k(s,a)^\top\bar\psi(g)/\sqrt d$
\State $y\gets r+\gamma mV(s',g)$ \Comment{$\gamma=0.99$}

\Statex \textbf{Phase 2: Representation \& Value Learning}
\State $\mathcal L_V\gets\ell_2^\tau\left(\bar q-\phi_V(s)^\top\psi(g)/\sqrt d\right)$ \Comment{$\tau=0.9$}
\State $\mathcal L_Q\gets\sum_k\left(\phi_k(s,a)^\top\psi(g)/\sqrt d-y\right)^2$
\State Update $\phi,\psi,\phi_V$ using $\mathcal L_V+\mathcal L_Q$

\Statex \textbf{Phase 3: Actor \& Flow Matching (Latent Interface)}
\State $z\gets\sg[\psi(g_\pi)]$
\State Sample $x_0\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$ and $t\sim U[0,1)$
\State $x_t\gets(1-t)x_0+ta$
\State $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{bc}}\gets\left|v_\theta(s,z,x_t,t)-(a-x_0)\right|^2$
\State Sample $\xi\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$
\State $\hat a\gets\pi_\eta(s,z,\xi)$
\State $\bar a\gets\sg[\textsc{FlowRollout}(v_\theta;s,z,\xi)]$
\State $q_\pi\gets\min_k\phi_k(s,\hat a)^\top\psi(g_\pi)/\sqrt d$
\State $\mathcal L_\pi\gets-\frac{\E[q_\pi]}{\sg[\E|Q|]+\epsilon}+\beta|\hat a-\bar a|^2+\mathcal L_{\mathrm{bc}}$ \Comment{$\beta=3$}
\State Update $\theta,\eta$ using $\mathcal L_\pi$

\Statex \textbf{Phase 4: Zero-shot inference (Test time)}
\State Sample offline states $\{s'_i\}_{i=1}^m$, with $m=50{,}000$, and evaluate $r_i=r(s'_i)\in\{0,1\}$
\State $z_{\mathrm{raw}}\gets\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^m r_i\psi(s'_i)$
\State $\bar n_\psi\gets\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^m|\psi(s'_i)|$
\State $z\gets z_{\mathrm{raw}}\frac{\bar n_\psi}{\max(|z_{\mathrm{raw}}|,\epsilon)}$
\State Act using $\pi_\eta(a\mid s,z)$ \Comment{the policy receives no explicit goal}

\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Centering the measure in FB}
\label{sec:centered}

Start from the $z$-independent bilinear successor-measure factorization used in our implementation and add just enough structure for the successor-measure TD loss to be well-behaved.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Start from the bilinear successor measure.}
    The measure $M(s,a,s')=\phi(s,a)^\top\tilde\psi(s')$, with $\tilde\psi=\sqrt d\,\psi/\|\psi\|$ and $\phi$ not conditioned on $z$ (as in our implementation), trained by the successor-measure TD loss
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal L_{\mathrm{meas}}
        = \frac{1}{2(B^2{-}B)}\sum_{i\neq j}\delta_{ij}^2 \;-\; \frac1B\sum_i \delta_{ii}
        \;+\; \lambda_\perp\,\mathcal L_\perp(\tilde\psi),
        \qquad
        \delta_{ij} = M_{ij} - \gamma\,\sg[\bar M_{ij}],
        \label{eq:fbloss}
    \end{equation}
    where the batch's own next-states $\{s'_j\}$ serve as measure test points, $\bar M_{ij}=\bar\phi(s'_i,a')^\top\bar{\tilde\psi}(s'_j)/\sqrt d$ with $a'$ drawn from the bootstrap policy, and $\mathcal L_\perp$ enforces orthonormality of $\tilde\psi$.
    On its own this objective fails for three reasons: its fixed point is low-performance (the measure loss alone reaches only $0.08$--$0.34$ on Cube), the learned-policy bootstrap $a'\sim\pi$ makes the backup non-contractive, and the factorization $\phi^\top\tilde\psi$ leaves the absolute scale non-identifiable.

    \item \textbf{Center the forward features with respect to the behavior policy.}
    Split the forward representation around its behavior-policy conditional feature mean $b(s)=\E_{a\sim\mu}[\phi(s,a)]$ (from $K$ flow samples):
    \begin{equation}
        \underbrace{\phi(s,a)^\top\tilde\psi(s')}_{M(s,a,s')} =\underbrace{b(s)^\top\tilde\psi(s')}_{\text{baseline }M_0(s,s')} \;+\;\underbrace{\big(\phi(s,a)-\sg\,b(s)\big)^\top\tilde\psi(s')}_{\text{residual }\Delta M(s,a,s')}.
    \end{equation}
    $M_0$ is an action-independent baseline component obtained by averaging the forward features under the behavior policy; $\Delta M$ is the action-dependent residual, a baseline/residual decomposition analogous to a dueling value decomposition.

    \item \textbf{Ground the measure with a fixed-policy backup.}
    In the pure-measure variant we train the full measure $M$ with a dataset-action, SARSA-style backup ($a'=a'_{\mathrm{data}}$), a $\gamma$-contraction, using an unnormalized objective that identifies the otherwise undetermined multiplicative scale of the factors.
    The learned-policy bootstrap is kept out of this term and confined to the residual (next step).

    \item \textbf{Downweight the policy-dependent residual.}
    Confine the learned-policy bootstrap $a'\sim\pi$ to $\Delta M$, at weight $\lambda\le1$, scale-normalized, with gradients propagated only to the backward representation $\psi$:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal L= \underbrace{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{meas}}(M;\,a'_{\mathrm{data}})}_{\text{fixed-policy, unnormalized}} \;+\;\lambda\,\underbrace{\mathcal L_{\Delta M}\big(a'{\sim}\pi\big)}_{\text{scale-normalized, }\lambda\le1} \;+\;\mathcal L_b\;+\;\mathcal L_\perp(\tilde\psi) .
    \end{equation}
    Here $\mathcal L_b$ fits $b$ to the behavior-policy feature mean and $\mathcal L_\perp$ enforces orthonormality of $\tilde\psi$; the actor maximizes the full $Q=\phi^\top\tilde\psi/\sqrt d$, normalized by $\sg|Q_{\mathrm{full}}|$.
    The contribution of the learned-policy bootstrap is explicitly controlled through scale normalization and the coefficient $\lambda$, and its gradients update only $\psi$.
    In the auxiliary variant the base term $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{meas}}(M;\,a'_{\mathrm{data}})$ is replaced by the GC-IQL loss.

    \item \textbf{Test-time inference is unchanged.}
    Same $z$ interface and reward-embedding estimation as in \S\ref{sec:rgfb}, so the method stays zero-shot.
\end{enumerate}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:centered} is the training step. The baseline regression (Phase 1) learns $b(s)$; the residual measure (Phase 2) is the only place the learned-policy bootstrap $a'\sim\pi$ appears. In the pure-measure variant (\texttt{only}, Phase 3) we train the full successor-measure factorization with a dataset-action, SARSA-style backup; this provides a fixed-behavior-policy successor-measure target and an unnormalized objective that identifies the scale, and the learned-policy bootstrap is retained only in the centered residual auxiliary term.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Centered successor-measure objective}
\label{alg:centered}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]

\Require bilinear features $\phi,\tilde\psi$, baseline head $b$, frozen behavior policy $\mu$, actor $\pi$, and target networks $\bar\phi,\bar{\tilde\psi}$

\Statex \textbf{Phase 1: Behavior-Policy Baseline Regression}
\State Sample $\tilde a_{1:K}\sim\mu(\cdot\mid s)$ using flow rollouts \Comment{$K=8$}
\State $\phi_\mu\gets\sg\left[\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K\phi(s,\tilde a_k)\right]$
\State $\mathcal L_b\gets\frac{|b(s)-\phi_\mu|^2}{\sg[\E(\phi^2)]+\epsilon}$

\Statex \textbf{Phase 2: Residual Measure Backup (Learned Policy)}
\State Sample $a'\sim\pi(\cdot\mid s')$
\State $\Delta\phi\gets\phi(s,a)-\sg[b(s)]$
\State $\Delta M\gets\frac{\sg[\Delta\phi]\tilde\psi(s')^\top}{\sqrt d}$
\State $\Delta M'\gets\sg\left[\frac{\left(\bar\phi(s',a')-b(s')\right)\bar{\tilde\psi}(s')^\top}{\sqrt d}\right]$
\State $\mathcal L_{\Delta M}\gets\textsc{MeasureTD}\left(\Delta M,\gamma\Delta M';\text{normalized}\right)$ \Comment{updates only $\tilde\psi$}

\Statex \textbf{Phase 3: Base Objective \& Total Loss}
\If{\texttt{centered\_measure=only}}
    \State $a_{\mathrm{base}}\gets a'_{\mathrm{data}}$ \Comment{dataset-action SARSA backup}
    \State $M\gets\frac{\phi(s,a)\tilde\psi(s')^\top}{\sqrt d}$
    \State $M'\gets\sg\left[\frac{\bar\phi(s',a_{\mathrm{base}})\bar{\tilde\psi}(s')^\top}{\sqrt d}\right]$
    \State $\mathcal L_0\gets\textsc{MeasureTD}\left(M,\gamma M';\text{unnormalized}\right)$
    \State $\mathcal L\gets\mathcal L_0+\lambda\mathcal L_{\Delta M}+\mathcal L_b+\mathcal L_\perp$
\ElsIf{\texttt{centered\_measure=aux}}
    \State $\mathcal L\gets\mathcal L_{\mathrm{IQL}}+\lambda\mathcal L_{\Delta M}+\mathcal L_b$
\EndIf

\Statex \textbf{Phase 4: Actor Update}
\State Update the actor using the full $Q_{\mathrm{full}}=\phi^\top\tilde\psi/\sqrt d$, normalized by $\sg[\E|Q_{\mathrm{full}}|]$

\Statex \textbf{Subroutine: \textsc{MeasureTD}}
\Statex For a batch measure matrix, define $\delta=M-\gamma M'$.
\Statex $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{off}}=\frac{1}{2(B^2-B)}\sum_{i\neq j}\delta_{ij}^2$.
\Statex $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{diag}}=-\frac{1}{B}\sum_i\delta_{ii}$.
\Statex $\textsc{MeasureTD}=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{off}}+\mathcal L_{\mathrm{diag}}$.
\Statex For the normalized variant, divide each term by $\sg[\sqrt{\E(M^2)}]+\epsilon$.

\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Empirical outcome}
\label{sec:centered-results}

Both variants were run on \texttt{cube-single-play} (state), 3 seeds, 500k steps.

The \textbf{auxiliary} variant (\texttt{aux}) is healthy: $0.708$--$0.780$ at 150k, tracking just
under the bilinear GC-IQL anchor. A centered measure term therefore \emph{coexists} with scalar
Bellman supervision without the collapse seen when the measure objective is added naively --- it
neither helps nor destroys.

The \textbf{pure-measure} variant (\texttt{only}) is \textbf{refuted}. A first attempt scale-normalized
both measure legs, which made the objective scale-invariant; with no scalar TD to pin the absolute
scale, the diagonal attraction term inflated without bound ($q$ mean $10^{8}\!\to\!10^{13}$) and the
run floored at $0.06$--$0.08$. We then restored FB's own anchors --- sphere-projected
$\tilde\psi$ ($\|\tilde\psi\|=\sqrt d$) throughout, \emph{raw} (unnormalized) regression on the
contractive SARSA base leg, and $\lambda_\perp=1$ --- which removed the runaway. This anchored run
peaked early at $0.18$--$0.26$ (50k), roughly $3\times$ the historical measure-only ceiling, but did
not hold: by 500k all three seeds had decayed back to $0.068$, $0.060$, $0.104$, i.e.\ the same
$0.076$--$0.088$ ceiling the uncentered measure objective reaches.

Centering, scale anchoring, and confining the learned-policy bootstrap to the residual therefore
\textbf{do not rescue the measure-native objective}. The transient early peak indicates the
construction does buy a better-behaved optimization \emph{path}, but the fixed point it converges to
is the same bad one. This is consistent with the annealing result that the measure's own fixed point
is poor on Cube and that scalar Bellman supervision --- not a better-conditioned measure loss --- is
the load-bearing ingredient. Under this evidence \S\ref{sec:rgfb}, which keeps the FB interface but
drops the measure objective entirely, is the variant that works ($0.825\pm0.014$ zero-shot).
