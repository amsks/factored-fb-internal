\section{Setup and anchors}

Goal: zero-shot RL for manipulation --- train once on unlabeled play data, solve any test task
without retraining. FB (forward--backward) is the standard method; on cube it gets
\numbad{0.42} (1M steps; 0.34 at 500k). One change to the supervised baseline GCIQL --- replace
the monolithic value network with the \emph{bilinear} form
\begin{equation}
Q(s,a,g) \;=\; \frac{\phi(s,a)^{\top}\psi(g)}{\sqrt d},
\qquad \phi,\psi:\ \text{MLPs}\ (512^3\!\to\!d)
\label{eq:bilinear}
\end{equation}
--- lifted it from 0.72 to \numgood{0.904} (0.947 on the matched-protocol re-eval), past the
published SOTA (0.68). But only with a \emph{flow} policy: with a gradient-climbing DDPG+BC
policy the same critic drops to \numbad{0.51} (the MLP critic: 0.70 either way). Explaining
this, and using the explanation to fix FB, is the project.

\section{The Socratic chain: ask why until nothing remains}

Each rung is a question, the experiment that answered it, and the number. Open ends are
marked \fbox{open}.

\whyq{does FB fail on cube --- architecture or objective?}
We built every hybrid: identical networks, swap only the training signal. FB's structural
pieces all survive under GCIQL's scalar-TD objective ($z$-indexed forward map 0.844; two-tower
0.864; the full FB structure stack 0.871). The identical architecture under FB's
successor-measure objective: \numbad{0.08--0.34}. Decisive version (\emph{anneal-iqlfade}):
initialize \emph{at} the 0.93 solution and fade the scalar TD out $\Rightarrow$ \numbad{0.05}.
\textbf{Answer: the objective --- its fixed point on cube is bad; it leaves the solution when
handed it.}

\whyq{is the objective the only broken part --- what about FB's task-inference API?}
Run FB's test-time rule on the trained bilinear GCIQL critic: $z=\sum_i r_i\,\psi(s'_i)$ over
50k reward-labeled dataset samples, feed $z$ to a $z$-conditioned policy: \numgood{0.955},
equal to exact-goal conditioning. \textbf{Answer: the API is intact; only the grounding is
broken.}

\whyq{does bilinear win --- is it out-of-distribution generalization?}
A $2\times2$ grid crosses start shift $\times$ goal shift (Fig.~\ref{fig:oodgrid}). FB fails
\emph{in distribution} (0.43 from dataset starts with hindsight goals, grasping 100\% of the
time --- its failure is post-grasp steering). The bilinear--MLP separation is the \emph{goal}
axis: novel goals are free for bilinear ($\Delta\!\approx\!+0.06$) and cost the MLP 16--24
points; start shift hurts both. \textbf{Answer: partially --- goal generalization is real, but
it cannot explain the on-support gap, so keep asking.}

\begin{figure}[h]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.72\textwidth]{fig_oodgrid}
\caption{The evaluation grid (C1$\to$C3). FB's C1 uses the $z{=}B(g)$ route; its C4 was not
run. The MLP is \emph{best} fully in-distribution (C1 = 0.98) and worst on the real eval.}
\label{fig:oodgrid}
\end{figure}

\whyq{does bilinear win on-support --- is it a more accurate value function?}
No --- it is \emph{less} accurate. Rank-AUC of value against realized success from in-hand
states (flow actor held fixed on both sides): MLP \numgood{0.81}, bilinear \numbad{0.39},
below chance. Decomposing $Q = U(s,g) + A(s,a,g)$, $U = \E_{a\sim\mu}Q$: the MLP's entire
signal is in the action-independent baseline (AUC 0.82); bilinear's $U$ is anti-calibrated
(0.40). \textbf{Answer: not accuracy. The two critics allocate capacity to different parts of
$Q$: MLP to the baseline, bilinear to the action-dependent part.}

\whyq{--- then what does the bilinear critic actually do better?}
Deploy the frozen critic as a pure ranker: sample $N$ candidates from a plain BC flow $\mu$
each step, execute $\arg\max_i Q(s,a_i,g)$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:rerank}). Bilinear:
\numgood{0.91--0.96} at $N{=}32$ --- equal to its fully trained actor, \emph{with no actor
training at all}. MLP: \numbad{0.57--0.59}. FB: \emph{declines} with $N$ (0.52 at $N{=}1$
$\to$ 0.17 at $N{=}32$) --- its scores actively mislead about actions. Fresh actors trained
against frozen critics reproduce the gap (0.88 vs 0.68), so it is the critic field, not
co-training. \textbf{Answer: the product is the on-support action ordering. The ordering
\emph{is} the policy.}

\whyq{does the gradient-climbing actor lose with a critic whose ordering is near-perfect?}
Because the ordering is only near-perfect \emph{on support}. The bilinear critic values the
DDPG+BC actor's fabricated action above \emph{every} realistic reference action 79\% of the
time (median $+2.14$), with action-gradient norms equal to the MLP's (3.18 vs 3.06): a
spurious off-support \emph{maximum}, not a steeper landscape. A $Q$-ascending Gaussian climbs
into it (fresh DDPG+BC vs frozen bilinear: 0.52); the distillation-anchored flow policy
never leaves the data manifold. \textbf{Answer: the critic's one flaw lives exactly where
gradient extraction looks and anchored extraction never does.}

\whyq{is that off-support peak intrinsic to the bilinear form?}
No --- it is domain-dependent (Fig.~\ref{fig:peak}): cube 63.9\% of states, antmaze
\numgood{0.3\%}, scene 1.4\% (which is why bilinear+DDPG+BC \emph{wins} antmaze at 0.837).
And it is \emph{not} explained by dataset action coverage: dimension-corrected, cube's
conditional action manifold is only $1.11\times$ narrower than antmaze's --- far too small
for a $0.639\to0.003$ flip. \textbf{Answer: the peak's location follows the domain's value
geometry, not the data.} \fbox{open}: a predictive account of \emph{where} the rank-$d$
form's irreducible error lands.

\whyq{does the bilinear form order actions well when the MLP orders them barely above chance?}
First hypothesis --- the MLP's action signal exists but is per-goal noise, and the
factorization merely pools/denoises it --- is \numbad{refuted} (Fig.~\ref{fig:t1}): averaging
the MLP's scores over local goal clouds makes it monotonically \emph{worse}
($0.616 \to 0.408$ at $K{=}32$, $\sigma{=}4$\,cm), and a 2--4\,cm goal shift is already
distribution shift for the MLP head (the same brittleness as its $-16$ to $-24$ point
goal-shift loss). So the MLP's action signal is \emph{absent}, not noisy.
Which-action-is-better is $<1\%$ of $Q$'s variance; the MLP can satisfy the training loss
while ignoring it. The surviving hypothesis: the factorization makes that impossible --- one
shared $\phi$ must serve every goal, so the tiny action signal accumulates in one basis at
training time. \textbf{Answer (partial): not denoising; constraint-forced structure.}
\fbox{open}: the causal control (same two towers, MLP readout instead of inner product; and a
param-matched MLP) is queued as T5.

Smoothing never helps the MLP and degrades it with radius; the random-goal control collapses
both (the signal is genuinely goal-specific).}
\label{fig:t1}
\end{figure}

\whyq{is the factorized form also more \emph{stable}, and why?}
Let the goal leg see the state --- $\psi(s,g)$ instead of $\psi(g)$ --- and 2 of 3 seeds
diverge late (value loss $\times$10--30, $Q$ crossing positive on a $\le 0$-bounded value).
The blow-up is localized in the state-conditioned leg: $\E\|\psi\|^2 = 1083/2355/3789$ across
seeds, monotone in divergence severity, $q_{\max}$ up to $+1.2\times10^3$, while $\phi$ stays
sane. \textbf{Answer: with $\psi(g)$ only, any score inflation must pass through globally
shared features and is corrected by the rest of the data; $\psi(s,g)$ opens a local $(s,g)$
pocket that expectile-TD's upward pressure inflates. The goal bottleneck is the stabilizer.}

\whyq{cube --- why does all of this matter most on this benchmark?}
Three coincidences of the domain: (i) the benchmark's binding distribution shift is the
\emph{goal} shift --- free for the factorization (Fig.~\ref{fig:oodgrid}); (ii) success hinges
on post-grasp, per-step action choice --- an ordering problem, not a value-magnitude problem
(all three methods reach at rate 1.0 and grasp within 6 points of each other; the entire
method spread is in $P(\mathrm{success}\mid\mathrm{secured})$: FB 0.44, MLP 0.80, bilinear
0.98); (iii) the play data's action distribution makes the imitation anchor a good one --- on
antmaze the same goal-agnostic anchor breaks the flow actor for \emph{both} critics
(0.24--0.33), an actor-side artifact we are fixing, not a fact about the critic.

\paragraph{Bedrock --- what remains open after all the whys.}
(1) \emph{Why} the low-rank constraint forces good ordering (T5 control queued).
(2) \emph{Where} the off-support peak forms --- a predictive value-geometry account (T3 ruled
out data coverage). (3) \emph{Scene}: bilinear loses there with both actors (0.26--0.29 vs
0.52) and shows no peak --- a different, undiagnosed mechanism. (4) Whether \emph{any}
measure-TD grounding can work on cube (T10, running --- \S\ref{sec:t10}).

\section{The answer, condensed}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \textbf{Sound grounding.} Scalar hindsight TD is a contractive backup evaluated only at
dataset actions, task-weighted where cube needs it. FB's measure TD is non-contractive,
occupancy-weighted (99.8\% of its $Q$ variance is state-driven), with a bad fixed point here.
\item \textbf{Capacity spent on ordering.} The bilinear form cannot ignore the tiny
($<1\%$-variance) action signal the way a monolithic net can; one shared $\phi$ serving all
goals accumulates it. Price: value calibration (irrelevant downstream) and one structured
off-support flaw. Bonus: free goal generalization and backup stability.
\item \textbf{Extraction that consumes exactly the good part.} Anchored (flow / selection)
extraction reads only the on-support ordering; gradient extraction reads the flaw.
\end{enumerate}

\section{GC-IQL grounding (shared by all working cells)}\label{sec:iql}
Rewards $r\in\{-1,0\}$ ($0$ at the goal), masks $m=1-\mathbf 1[s'{=}g]$, expectile $\tau=0.9$,
twin critics, target network $\bar Q$:
\begin{align}
\mathcal L_V &= \E\big[\,\ell_\tau^2\big(\min_i \bar Q_i(s,a,g) - V(s,g)\big)\big],
&& \ell_\tau^2(u) = |\tau - \mathbf 1[u<0]|\,u^2,\\
\mathcal L_Q &= \textstyle\sum_i \E\big[\big(Q_i(s,a,g) - (r + \gamma\, m\, V(s',g))\big)^2\big].
\end{align}
Value goals: 0.2 current / 0.5 geometric future ($p{=}1{-}\gamma$) / 0.3 random; actor goals:
uniform future on the same trajectory.

\section{The math of every FB variant we ran}\label{sec:variants}

\subsection{Canonical FB, as ported (torch; \texttt{agents/fb/})}
Measure $M^{\pi_z}(s,a,\cdot)\approx F(s,z,a)^\top B(\cdot)\,\rho(\cdot)$;
$Q(s,a,z)=F(s,z,a)^\top z$; $\|B\|=\|z\|=\sqrt d$ (hard norm). With batch goals
$\{s'_j\}\sim\rho$ and $a'\sim\pi_z(s')$:
\begin{align}
\Delta_{ij} &= F(s_i,z,a_i)^\top B(s'_j) - \gamma\,\sg\!\big[\bar F(s'_i,z,a')^\top \bar B(s'_j)\big],\\
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FB}} &= \frac{1}{2(B^2{-}B)}\sum_{i\neq j}\Delta_{ij}^2 \;-\;
\frac1B\sum_i \Delta_{ii}
\;+\; \lambda_\perp \Big[\tfrac{1}{2(B^2-B)}\textstyle\sum_{i\neq j}(B_i^\top B_j)^2 - \overline{B_i^\top B_i}\Big].
\end{align}
$z$-mix: 50\% uniform on the $\sqrt d$ sphere, 50\% $\mathrm{proj}_{\sqrt d} B(s'_{\mathrm{perm}})$.
Actor = FlowBC ($z$-conditioned one-shot actor distilled from a goal-agnostic BC flow, $-Q$
term normalized by $\sg|Q|$). Inference: $z=\mathrm{proj}_{\sqrt d}\big(\sum_i r_i B(s'_i)\big)$,
10k relabeled samples. \textbf{Result: 0.42 (1M) / 0.34 (500k).} Audited port deltas vs canon:
twin targets aggregated by \emph{mean}, not min (an optimism delta --- the exact failure mode
the conservative-FB literature diagnoses on narrow data); the ortho diagonal is inert (the
sphere norm makes it constant); a learned left-encoder $L(s)$ feeds $F$.

\subsection{RG-FB (this week): FB's API on the scalar grounding}
Keep the FB interface, swap the grounding. Training: Eq.~\eqref{eq:bilinear} +
\S\ref{sec:iql} with $z=\psi(g)$; actor = FQL-style flow conditioned on $\sg[\psi(g)]$,
\begin{align}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{vf}} &= \E\,\|v(s, c, x_t, t) - (a - x_0)\|^2,\qquad x_t = (1{-}t)x_0 + t\,a,\\
\mathcal L_\pi &= -\,\E\big[\min\nolimits_i Q_i(s, \pi(s,z,\varepsilon), g)\big]/\sg|Q|
\;+\; 3.0\,\|\pi(s,z,\varepsilon) - \mathrm{Euler}_{10}(v; s, c, \varepsilon)\|^2,
\end{align}
with vf conditioning $c\in\{\varnothing, z\}$ under test. \textbf{Zero-shot inference}
(the deployed policy never sees $g$):
\begin{equation}
z \;=\; \sum_i r_i\,\psi(s'_i)\ \ \text{over 50k relabeled dataset samples},\qquad
z \leftarrow z\cdot \frac{\overline{\|\psi(s')\|}}{\|z\|}.
\end{equation}
\textbf{Measured today} (Fig.~\ref{fig:rgfb}): zero-shot \numgood{$0.825\pm0.014$}, exact-$z$
0.852 --- inference gap 2.7 points, inside the pre-registered $\le$3-point band (the API is
clean); the $\sqrt d$ norm instead of the matched norm collapses to 0.365 (norm hygiene is
load-bearing, confirmed three times). The $c=\varnothing$ control is tracking \emph{above} v0
(0.872 at 300k) --- the goal-agnostic anchor appears load-bearing on cube. Generality probe
(on the $d{=}512$/1M checkpoint): novel region-goals inside the data support \numgood{0.672}
(any-goal-position zero-shot is real; the single-goal anchors reproduce at 0.952); two-goal
composite rewards \numbad{0.26} --- the blended $z$ leaves the trained manifold. The v1 fix
is task-mixture hindsight relabeling: sample goals $g_{1..K}$, weights $w$, reward
$\sum_k w_k\mathbf 1[s{\approx}g_k]$, $z=\sum_k w_k\psi(g_k)$, same TD.

\subsection{Centered (advantage) measure --- the measure-native rescue (running)}\label{sec:t10}
Motivation: control needs the action-contingent $\Delta M$, but the raw measure loss is
dominated by the occupancy baseline ($\Delta M \sim 0.2\%$ of $Q$ variance). Give it its own
objective. With $b(s)$ regressed to the behavior-mean feature and $\tilde\psi$ sphere-pinned:
\begin{align}
\mathcal L_b &= \big\|\,b(s) - \sg\big[\tfrac1K\textstyle\sum_k \phi(s,\tilde a_k)\big]\big\|^2
\big/\, \sg[\overline{\phi_\mu^2}],\qquad \tilde a_k \sim \mu,\ K{=}8,\\
\Delta M_{ij} &= \big(\phi(s_i,a_i) - \sg[b(s_i)]\big)^\top \tilde\psi(s'_j)/\sqrt d,\qquad
\tilde\psi = \sqrt d\,\psi/\|\psi\|.
\end{align}
\textbf{T10-a (auxiliary)}: $\mathcal L = \mathcal L_{\mathrm{IQL}} + \lambda(\mathcal
L_{\Delta M} + \mathcal L_b)$, with $\mathcal L_{\Delta M}$ the FB off-diag/diag form on
$\Delta M - \gamma\,\sg[\Delta \bar M(s', a'{\sim}\pi,\cdot)]$, scale-normed.
\emph{Status: healthy at 0.71--0.78 mid-training --- the first FB-style loss that does not
hurt on cube.}\\[2pt]
\textbf{T10-b2 (measure-only, anchored, split bootstrap)}: no scalar TD anywhere.
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L = \underbrace{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{meas}}\big(M,\ a'{=}a'_{\mathrm{data}}\big)}_{\text{raw, contractive (SARSA base leg)}}
\;+\; \lambda\,\underbrace{\mathcal L_{\Delta M}\big(a'{\sim}\pi\big)}_{\text{scale-normed}}
\;+\; \mathcal L_b \;+\; \mathcal L_\perp(\tilde\psi),
\end{equation}
policy improvement confined to the (small) $\Delta M$ leg; the actor always consumes the full
$Q=\phi^\top\tilde\psi/\sqrt d$ normalized by $\sg|Q_{\mathrm{full}}|$ (never $\Delta M$ alone
--- that pattern floored the dueling cell). \emph{Status: the un-anchored first attempt was
scale-invariant and inflated without bound ($Q$ to $10^{13}$; diagnosed, fixed); the anchored
relaunch has $\|\tilde\psi\|^2$ pinned at exactly $d$, the $\Delta M$ leg flat, the $M$ scale
decelerating; first evals landing now. Pre-registered: $\ge$0.5 = a real measure-native
rescue (best prior measure-only: 0.088); jointly with faithful one-step FB, failure licenses
``no known measure-TD grounding supports cube.''}

\section{Claims we would defend in a paper}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
\item FB-style zero-shot RL fails on manipulation because of its \emph{training objective}
(non-contractive, occupancy-weighted, bad fixed point) --- not its architecture, not its
task-inference interface; and the failure is manipulation-specific (the same objective scores
0.784 on antmaze, where occupancy \emph{is} the task).
\item A goal-factored bilinear head turns goal-conditioned TD into an action-ranking machine:
state-of-the-art manipulation under imitation-anchored extraction, with measured failure
conditions (gradient extraction; domains where the off-support peak appears).
\item RG-FB: FB's zero-shot interface on scalar grounding reaches supervised-level cube
performance zero-shot (0.825 today, isolation control pending), with a measured claim
boundary (arbitrary goal positions: yes, 0.672 on never-evaluated regions; composite rewards:
not yet, 0.26 --- fix identified).
\end{enumerate}
