\section*{Datasets}

Every dataset above is one packed \texttt{npz} per task at
\texttt{<root>/data/<family>/<task>.npz}, the eight unified fields stacked $[N,T,\cdot]$
(\texttt{observation}, \texttt{action}, \texttt{next\_observation},
\texttt{achieved\_goal}, \texttt{reward}, \texttt{terminal}, \texttt{timeout},
\texttt{success}). \texttt{env/unified.py::load\_packed} flattens to $N\!\cdot\!T$
transitions and \emph{synthesizes} the \texttt{terminals} that mark trajectory boundaries
at each episode's last step: the env's own \texttt{terminal} flag never fires, since every
collection here is fixed-horizon. Two schema facts matter for reading the tables.
(i)~\texttt{achieved\_goal} is a zeros placeholder in every pack and is never read.
(ii)~The eval goal is \emph{not} taken from the data's goal field. The original loader
reused a single fixed frame --- the highest-reward \texttt{next\_observation} in the whole
dataset --- for every eval episode, which fed every goal-conditioned critic the same wrong
goal and is the defect the repaired protocol removes. The standard now is a
\emph{per-episode synthesized eval goal}: the episode's own target is written into the
masked goal frame, so the critic is evaluated against the goal the episode is actually
being scored on. Every result in this document uses that synthesized eval.

Statistics in Table~\ref{tab:datasets} were recomputed from the packed files rather than
copied from the collection logs, except for the \texttt{play-resample} v3 rows, whose
content statistics are carried by the collector changelog instead (see the caption).
\emph{ep-succ} is the fraction of episodes in which
\texttt{success} ever fires; \emph{dwell} is the fraction of \emph{all} steps spent at the
goal --- the quantity that separates ``reached it'' from ``sat on it''.

\paragraph{Sample budget.}
The \texttt{trans} column used to be the single most under-reported difference between our
arms and the OGBench reference: every Metaworld and ManiSkill result was trained on
$2.5\times10^4$--$10^5$ transitions against OGBench's $1.00\times10^6$, a 10--40$\times$
gap. Table~\ref{tab:e1} shows why that gap was not a detail --- the architectural effect
under study does not exist below ${\sim}10^5$ transitions and is only fully formed by
$2.5\times10^5$, so those arms were measuring sample budget and reporting it as
architecture. Every cited row above is now at OGBench scale, and the comparison is
finally apples-to-apples. One residual asymmetry remains: OGBench ships a held-out
validation split ($100{,}100$ transitions per env) that our packs have no counterpart for;
\texttt{env/unified.py} instead carves a \texttt{val\_frac} tail out of the same
trajectories.

\paragraph{\texttt{play-resample} v3 (Metaworld).}
From \texttt{data\_collection/collect\_mw\_play\_resample.py}: 2000 trajectories of 500
wrapper steps ($=1000$ simulator steps, cube-play scale, $1.00$M transitions per task).
The target is re-drawn from the env's own \texttt{goal\_space} and written into
\texttt{raw.\_target\_pos} --- exactly the quantity the observation's goal dims
$\texttt{obs}[36{:}39]$ and the scripted oracle read --- on a fixed timer with
reject-and-retry on the drawn target, so no segment is stuck and no target is
unreachable. \texttt{raw.curr\_path\_length} is zeroed before each wrapper step to defeat
Metaworld's $\texttt{max\_path\_length}=500$ truncation. Coverage comes from relocation
chaining rather than action noise, so the mixture is deliberately gentle; each trajectory
becomes a chain of reach-and-move segments, and within-trajectory relabeling therefore
crosses target contexts through transitions the data actually contains. v3 supersedes v1
(archived as \texttt{play-resample-v1}) on four counts: $2000\times500$ instead of
$200\times500$, timer-only re-draw, goal-channel staleness fixed on 7--10\% of
transitions, and the removal of below-table push targets and degenerate one-step segments.
Full changelog in \texttt{hf\_dataset/README\_play.md}.

\paragraph{\texttt{ms-play-resample} (ManiSkill).}
Regenerated at cube scale so that E2 (Table~\ref{tab:e2}) is size-matched to
\texttt{cube-single}: TD-MPC2 goal-resample rollouts with the target re-drawn on success,
$\sigma=0.05$, $8000\times125$ for pick and $20000\times50$ for push, $1.00$M transitions
each. The two halves are structurally different on purpose. Pick carries $11.0$
relocations per trajectory at dwell $0.279$ --- genuine goal diversity. Push carries
essentially one target per trajectory, because \texttt{ms-push-cube}'s goal is
deterministic given the cube pose: there is nothing for a re-draw to vary. That is why
push is entered as a \emph{declared control} rather than a test cell, and why its
goal-blind BC saturates in Table~\ref{tab:e2}.
