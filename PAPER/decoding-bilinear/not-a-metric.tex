\paragraph{Scope.} This note isolates one result of the decoding-bilinear project: the
refutation of the \emph{metric hypothesis} for bilinear GCIQL (\biql) on OGBench
\texttt{cube-single-play-v0}, with every number from our own probe matrix
(3 seeds $\times$ 500k steps per condition; probe battery of
\texttt{docs/specs/2026-07-31-bilinear-gciql-metric-probe.md}). It ends
(\S\ref{sec:instead}) with the mathematical argument for the surviving alternative.
Companion notes: \texttt{phenomenon.tex} (pre-registered phenomena and kill-tests),
\texttt{decoding-bilinear.tex} (kernel formalism).

\section{The hypothesis under test}

The critic is $\hat Q(s,a,g)=\ip{\varphi(s,a)}{\psi(g)}/\sqrt d$ with
$\hat V(s,g)=\ip{\varphi(s)}{\psi(g)}/\sqrt d$, trained by goal-conditioned expectile TD
($\tau=0.9$, $\gamma=0.99$) on relabeled play data. Let $d^*(s,g)$ denote the minimal
dataset-supported hitting time and, for any critic construction, let
$\hat d(s,g)$ be its induced distance (for \biql: $\hat d = \log_\gamma(1+(1-\gamma)\hat V)$,
or the self-subtracted logit $f(g,g)-f(s,g)$; both are monotone reparametrizations of
$\hat V$ and rank-equivalent for every probe below).

\begin{hypothesis}[Metric hypothesis, H-metric]\label{hyp:metric}
\biql's policy advantage over the monolithic \gciql\ ($92.7\pm1.4$ vs.\ $73.2\pm1.3$
success at 500k; pure-BC floor $8$--$10$) is mediated by the \emph{fidelity of its learned
geometry}: $\hat d_{\biql}$ approximates $d^*$ (equivalently, embeds a temporal metric)
better than $\hat d_{\gciql}$, and this fidelity causes the better guidance.
\end{hypothesis}

If H-metric holds, \biql\ must (a) rank temporal distances at least as well as \gciql\ on
held-out pairs, (b) agree geometrically with the architecturally metric-constrained
references (\qrl; and \crl's contrastive embedding), and (c) improve its geometry over the
training window in which success improves.

\section{The evidence: all three implications fail}

\begin{finding}[Temporal ranking: \gciql\ is the better metric, and loses]\label{f:p1}
Probe P1, Spearman $\rho(\hat d, \Delta t)$ over $10^5$ same-trajectory validation pairs
(second number: partial correlation controlling raw-observation $L_2$):
\[
\gciql:\; 0.63\,/\,0.54 \qquad>\qquad \biql:\; 0.54\,/\,0.41 .
\]
The critic with the \emph{worse} temporal geometry wins by 20 success points.
Fidelity of $\hat d$ to $d^*$ on the data distribution is therefore not sufficient
(\gciql) and not necessary (\biql) for guidance value.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}[Representational agreement: \biql\ is the geometric outlier]\label{f:rsa}
RSA (Spearman between upper triangles of the $2000\times2000$ native distance matrices):
$\mathrm{RSA}(\gciql,\qrl)=0.88$, $\mathrm{RSA}(\gciql,\crl)=0.54$ --- a three-method
\emph{consensus geometry} among the metric-flavoured methods. \biql\ agrees with none of
them ($\le 0.21$ against every learned method) and sits closest to \emph{raw observation
distance} ($0.45$--$0.52$). If \biql\ had learned the temporal metric, it would join the
consensus; instead the consensus members underperform it by 20--60 points.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}[The cosine structure is architectural, not learned]\label{f:cos}
The trained head is numerically a scaled cosine
($\mathrm{corr}(\ip{\varphi}{\psi},\cos\angle(\varphi,\psi))=0.996$; norm CVs
$\le 0.09$) --- but the \emph{untrained} network already has cos-correlation $0.994$:
LayerNorm concentrates tower norms at initialization. ``It learned a cosine similarity''
is a property of the architecture class, not of training, and cannot explain a
training-acquired advantage.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}[Temporal dissociation: geometry saturates, success does not]\label{f:time}
From 100k to 500k steps, \biql's P1-partial moves $0.37\to0.41$ while success moves
$65\to93$. The quantity H-metric says is causal is flat over the window in which the
effect it must explain triples.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}[Where fidelity actually differs: eval-task rollouts, with the sign
reversed]\label{f:p2}
Probe P2 (2026-08-03): per-episode Spearman $\rho(\hat V(s_t,g),\,t)$ along temperature-0
eval rollouts, full $2\times2$ of scoring critic $\times$ rollout-generating policy
(3 seeds $\times$ 50 episodes; median over episodes, mean$\pm$sd over seeds):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
scored on $\to$ & \biql-policy rollouts ($0.95$ succ.) & \gciql-policy rollouts ($0.73$ succ.)\\
\midrule
\biql\ critic  & $0.983\pm0.003$ & $0.871\pm0.060$\\
\gciql\ critic & $\mathbf{0.355\pm0.239}$ & $0.883\pm0.015$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
On val-set pairs \gciql\ ranks better (Finding~\ref{f:p1}); on the eval-task states of
the \emph{successful} policy its value signal is noise (per-task medians as low as
$-0.80$), while \biql's is a near-perfect monotone ramp. Each critic is coherent along its
own policy's trajectories (diagonal); only \biql\ \emph{transfers} to the other policy's
state distribution. So the fidelity that discriminates the two critics is not metric
fidelity on the data distribution --- it is \emph{off-distribution coherence} on exactly
the $(s,g)$ pairs eval interrogates.
\end{finding}

\begin{remark}[Verdict]
H-metric is refuted on every implication: (a) fails by Finding~\ref{f:p1}, (b) by
Findings~\ref{f:rsa}--\ref{f:cos}, (c) by Finding~\ref{f:time}; and
Finding~\ref{f:p2} locates the real discriminator elsewhere. \biql\ also violates metric
axioms freely where it wins (asymmetry index and triangle-violation rates comparable to
\gciql's in the trust region, magnitudes bounded) --- metric \emph{axioms} are neither
enforced nor selected for. Whatever \biql\ learns, calling it a metric explains none of
the observed orderings.
\end{remark}

\section{What the critic is plausibly doing instead}\label{sec:instead}

The surviving account is that the bilinear head is a \textbf{bounded low-rank completion
of the in-sample-optimal reachability kernel}, and that its advantage is a property of the
\emph{function class at the actor interface}, not of any geometry it encodes. We give the
argument in three steps, each attached to a measured quantity.

\paragraph{Setup.} Let $K^*(s,g)=\gamma^{d^*(s,g)}\in[0,1]$ and let
$V^*=(K^*-1)/(1-\gamma)$ be the optimal goal-conditioned value under the $-1$-per-step
convention, so $V^*\in[-1/(1-\gamma),\,0]=[-100,0]$ at $\gamma=0.99$. Expectile TD at
$\tau\to1$ regresses toward the in-sample optimal $V^*$ on the relabeled pair measure
$\mu$. The bilinear class with LayerNorm towers is
\[
\mathcal F_d \;=\;\Big\{(s,g)\mapsto \tfrac{1}{\sqrt d}\ip{\varphi(s)}{\psi(g)} \;:\;
\|\varphi\|\approx\kappa_\varphi,\ \|\psi\|\approx\kappa_\psi\Big\},
\qquad \kappa:=\kappa_\varphi\kappa_\psi/\sqrt d .
\]
Measured on \biql-$d50$: $\kappa\approx75$, realized $\hat V\in[-63,\,+6]$.

\paragraph{Step 1 (boundedness caps actor exploitation).}
The one-step actor maximizes $\hat Q$ against a BC anchor. For any policy $\pi$ reached by
ascent, the standard decomposition gives
\[
J(\pi)\;\ge\;J(\pi_{\mathrm{BC}})\;+\;\underbrace{\E_{s,g}\big[\hat Q(s,\pi)-\hat Q(s,\pi_{\mathrm{BC}})\big]}_{\text{what ascent collects}}
\;-\;2\sup_{s,a,g}\big|\hat Q-Q^*\big| .
\]
For an unbounded monolithic MLP the sup is uncontrolled precisely off the data
distribution, where ascent goes hunting; within $\mathcal F_d$ it is at most
$\kappa+\sup|Q^*|$, i.e.\ the class cannot manufacture value spikes: since
$|\hat Q|\le\kappa\approx 75<100=\sup|V^*|$, the class cannot even \emph{represent} the
most extreme values, let alone hallucinate beyond them. Errors are forced to be
\emph{many, small, and spread} rather than rare and enormous --- exactly the measured
triangle-violation profile (\biql\ magnitude $4.8$ steps vs.\ \gciql\ $9.5$ at comparable
rates).

\paragraph{Step 2 (rank $d$ forces completion to unseen pairs).}
Write the value table on supports $\mathcal S\times\mathcal G$ as a matrix
$M_{sg}=\hat V(s,g)$; $\mathcal F_d$ constrains $\operatorname{rank}(M)\le d$. The training
measure $\mu$ covers a sub-block pattern $\Omega\subset\mathcal S\times\mathcal G$
(each state with \emph{some} goals, each goal with \emph{some} states --- play relabeling
guarantees marginal coverage, not joint coverage). A rank-$d$ matrix has
$d(|\mathcal S|+|\mathcal G|-d)$ degrees of freedom, and under standard incoherence
conditions is \emph{uniquely determined} by $O\big(d\,(|\mathcal S|+|\mathcal G|)\,
\mathrm{polylog}\big)$ generically positioned entries. Hence, once $\mu$ pins the factors
on $\Omega$, the values on the \emph{unmeasured} block
$(\mathcal S\times\mathcal G)\setminus\Omega$ --- which contains the eval pairs
$(s,g^{\mathrm{task}})$ --- are not free parameters: the class extrapolates them by
completion, $\hat V = \Phi\Psi^\top/\sqrt d$ with both factors identified on the covered
marginals. A monolithic MLP obeys no such constraint; its off-$\Omega$ values are limited
only by smoothness. This is Finding~\ref{f:p2} in mathematical form: the $2\times2$
transfer asymmetry (coherent at $0.87$ off its training policy's distribution vs.\
\gciql's $0.36$) is the completion property, observed.

\paragraph{Step 3 (what is learned is a spectral object, not a metric).}
The expectile regression solves, in the idealized linear-algebraic limit,
\[
\min_{\operatorname{rank} M\le d}\ \big\|W_\tau\odot(M-V^*)\big\|_{\mu}^2 ,
\]
a $\tau$-weighted low-rank approximation of $V^*$: the learned object is (a reweighting
of) the \textbf{top-$d$ spectral subspace of the optimal value kernel}, not a distance
function. This predicts, and the data confirm:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item performance depends on capturing the top spectral subspace, so success saturates
once $d$ exceeds the kernel's effective spectral dimension: measured saturation at
$d^\dagger\in[10,20]$ ($0.06/0.62/0.92/0.90/0.93$ at $d=5/10/20/30/50$), with learned
effective rank tracking the container sublinearly ($4.1/8.0/14.7/19.6/28.0$) --- a
capacity budget, not a learned constant;
\item metric axioms (symmetry, triangle inequality) are epiphenomenal --- a truncated
spectral approximation of $\gamma^{d^*}$ satisfies neither exactly, and \biql\ indeed
violates both while winning;
\item geometry probes on $\mu$ (P1, RSA) can prefer \gciql\ while the actor prefers
\biql, because they measure pointwise ranking on $\Omega$, which is blind to both
boundedness (Step~1) and off-$\Omega$ completion (Step~2).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{proposition}[Value-completion account, falsifiable summary]
\biql's guidance advantage $=$ bounded ($\kappa<\sup|V^*|$), rank-$d^\dagger$ completion
of the expectile-optimal reachability kernel from marginal coverage. It predicts:
(a) bounding a monolithic critic at $\kappa$ recovers part of the $73\to93$ gap
(\texttt{gciql-qb75}, running); (b) destroying norm concentration degrades \biql\ at fixed
rank (\texttt{biql-d50-nopsiln}, running); (c) the completion behaviour of
Finding~\ref{f:p2} should survive any monotone reparametrization of $\hat V$ and vanish
for $d<d^\dagger$. Any of these failing bounds how much of the account survives; none of
them resurrects H-metric, which is refuted by Findings~\ref{f:p1}--\ref{f:p2}
independently.
\end{proposition}
