\begin{abstract}
Bilinear GCIQL --- expectile TD learning on a factored critic
$Q(s,a,g)=\ip{\varphi(s,a)}{\psi(g)}/\sqrt d$ --- reaches $92.7\%$ on OGBench
\texttt{cube-single-play}, twenty points above the same objective with a monolithic MLP
critic. We ask two questions: \emph{what property is the bilinear form leveraging}, and
\emph{does it transfer across environments}? The theory half of this note shows that the
critic's training target is a bounded quasimetric and that the bilinear head is a low-rank
factorization of the discounted reachability kernel $K^*=\gamma^{d^*}$, which places
bilinear GCIQL, Contrastive RL, VIP, and Quasimetric RL in one family. The experimental
half then rejects the natural reading of that theory: probed against methods that provably
learn a temporal metric, the bilinear critic has the \emph{weaker} distance --- the
monolithic critic tracks temporal structure better and agrees with QRL and CRL (RSA
$0.88$/$0.54$) while the bilinear critic agrees with nobody --- yet it is the better policy.
A selector experiment isolates what it does have: with \emph{no actor training}, executing
the best of $N$ actions from a frozen behavior-cloned flow under the bilinear critic's
ranking reproduces the trained policy at $N{=}8$ ($0.92$ vs.\ $0.93$), where the monolithic
critic needs $N{=}64$ and contrastive RL ranks at chance. The same ordering advantage
appears on Metaworld in every matched comparison, inside a selection channel whose
\emph{sign} is set by the environment's goal observability. The mechanism that survives
ablation is rank-restricted \emph{completion}: the expressed value function has a
capacity-independent $\approx$10-dimensional spectrum matching the environment's own
reachability kernel, and it remains coherent on states beyond the region where it was fit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Offline goal-conditioned RL on OGBench \texttt{cube-single-play-v0} produced a result we
did not expect to be interesting: replacing GCIQL's monolithic value MLP with a bilinear
head lifts success from $73.2\pm1.6$ to $92.7\pm1.7$ (500k steps, 3 seeds, flow-matching
actor with a one-step value term), while behavior cloning alone reaches $9\%$. Nothing
else changes --- objective, expectile, discount, networks, data, actor. Two questions
follow.

\textbf{RQ1: does this transfer?} Across a 288-run campaign on Metaworld and ManiSkill
(expert and noisy-expert data), it does not: the bilinear and monolithic critics tie
almost everywhere, and on ManiSkill the critic's contribution to the actor is negative for
every goal-conditioned method.

\textbf{RQ2: what is the bilinear form leveraging?} The obvious hypothesis --- the factored
critic implicitly learns a similarity or metric over states, as VIP, QRL, and CRL do by
construction --- is the one this note was originally written to formalize, and the one our
own experiments then rejected.

The paper is organized as theory (\S\ref{sec:setup}--\ref{sec:family}), experiments
(\S\ref{sec:experiments}--\ref{sec:selector}), and mechanism (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}--%
\ref{sec:transfer}). The full pre-registered phenomenon/kill-test log, including several
informative failures, lives in the companion note \texttt{phenomenon.tex}; here we keep
the results that survived.

\section{Setup}\label{sec:setup}

Consider a goal-conditioned MDP with state space $\mathcal S$, deterministic dynamics (as
in OGBench cube when the full state is observed), sparse reward $r(s;g)=\mathbf 1[s{=}g]-1$,
termination on reaching the goal, and discount $\gamma$. Let $d^*(s,g)\in\{0,1,\dots,\infty\}$
be the minimum number of steps needed to reach $g$ from $s$ under policies supported by the
offline dataset. The optimal goal-conditioned value is
\begin{equation}
V^*(s,g) \;=\; -\frac{1-\gamma^{d^*(s,g)}}{1-\gamma}, \qquad \gamma^{\infty}:=0 .
\label{eq:vstar}
\end{equation}
$d^*$ is a \emph{quasimetric}: $d^*(s,s)=0$ and
$d^*(s,g)\le d^*(s,w)+d^*(w,g)$, while symmetry is not required (Wang et al.\ 2023,
Thm.~1: quasimetrics are exactly the class of optimal goal-reaching values). Bilinear
GCIQL parametrizes $V(s,g)=\ip{\varphi(s)}{\psi(g)}/\sqrt d$ and
$Q(s,a,g)=\ip{\varphi(s,a)}{\psi(g)}/\sqrt d$, trained with expectile TD learning; as the
expectile $\tau\to1$, $V$ approaches $V^*$ over dataset-supported actions.

\section{The negated value is a quasimetric}

\begin{proposition}[Discounted quasimetric]\label{prop:qm}
Let $\phi_\gamma(x):=\frac{1-\gamma^x}{1-\gamma}$. Then
$D_\gamma:=-V^*=\phi_\gamma\circ d^*$ is a bounded quasimetric
($D_\gamma\le\frac{1}{1-\gamma}$).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\phi_\gamma$ is increasing, concave, and satisfies $\phi_\gamma(0)=0$; therefore it is
subadditive, $\phi_\gamma(a+b)\le\phi_\gamma(a)+\phi_\gamma(b)$. Thus
$\phi_\gamma(d^*(s,g))\le\phi_\gamma(d^*(s,w))+\phi_\gamma(d^*(w,g))$, and
$D_\gamma(s,g)=0\iff d^*(s,g)=0$. This is a metric-preserving transformation; the same
argument appears implicitly in Wang et al.\ 2023.
\end{proof}

The training target of bilinear GCIQL is therefore already a quasimetric. Equivalently,
define the \textbf{discounted goal-reachability kernel}
\begin{equation}
K^*(s,g) := \gamma^{d^*(s,g)} \;=\; 1+(1-\gamma)\,V^*(s,g)\;\in\;[0,1].
\label{eq:kernel}
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Composition inequality]\label{cor:mult}
$K^*(s,s)=1$ and $K^*(s,g)\ge K^*(s,w)\,K^*(w,g)$ for all $s,w,g$.
\end{corollary}

\section{Bilinear GCIQL as a low-rank factorization of $K^*$}\label{sec:lowrank}

Substituting the bilinear parametrization into \eqref{eq:kernel}, with
$\tilde\varphi(s):=\big(1,\tfrac{1-\gamma}{\sqrt d}\varphi(s)\big)$ and
$\tilde\psi(g):=\big(1,\psi(g)\big)$,
\begin{equation}
K(s,g) \;=\; \ip{\tilde\varphi(s)}{\tilde\psi(g)}, \qquad
\operatorname{rank}\le d+1 .
\label{eq:factorization}
\end{equation}
Bilinear GCIQL can represent the optimal reachability kernel exactly only when
$\gamma^{d^*}$ admits a rank-$d$ factorization, and approximately when it admits an
accurate rank-$d$ approximation --- a condition on the singular-value spectrum of the
state--goal reachability matrix, not on optimization. The discount controls how quickly
reachability decays with distance ($K^*=e^{-\beta d^*}$, $\beta=\ln(1/\gamma)$): larger
$\gamma$ compresses variation across temporal distances and can reduce effective rank.
\S\ref{sec:mechanism} makes this quantitative: the environment's own kernel has an
$\approx$10-dimensional spectrum, and the learned critic expresses exactly that spectrum
at every capacity we trained.

\section{One family: CRL, VIP, QRL}\label{sec:family}

\paragraph{CRL.} At the optimum of its binary NCE objective, the CRL critic
$f(s,a,g)=\ip{\varphi(s,a)}{\psi(g)}$ satisfies (Eysenbach et al.\ 2022, Lem.~4.1)
$\exp f^*=p^\pi_\gamma(s_+{=}g\mid s,a)/p(g)$ --- a discounted state-occupancy ratio.
Under deterministic dynamics with first hitting time $T^\pi(s,g)$ and no revisits,
$p^\pi_\gamma(g|s)=(1-\gamma)\gamma^{T^\pi(s,g)}$, so
\begin{equation}
f^*(s,g) \;=\; -\ln\!\tfrac1\gamma\; T^{\pi}(s,g) + c(g), \qquad
c(g)=\ln(1-\gamma)-\ln p(g).
\label{eq:crl}
\end{equation}
Both methods use a rank-$d$ bilinear head for quantities derived from
$\gamma^{T(s,g)}$, but with different links and different policies: CRL represents
$\log K^\pi+c(g)$ (behavior policy, log link, per-goal offset); GCIQL represents $K^*$
(optimal over dataset-supported actions, affine link, no offset). Myers et al.\ (2024)
recover a quasimetric from occupancy critics by self-subtraction
$d_{\mathrm{SD}}(s,g):=f(g,g)-f(s,g)$, which removes $c(g)$; applied to GCIQL, where
$V^*(g,g)=0$, self-subtraction returns $-V^*=D_\gamma$, so the two constructions coincide
and Prop.~\ref{prop:qm} applies at the optimum.

\paragraph{VIP.} VIP hard-codes $V(s;g)=-\lVert\phi(s)-\phi(g)\rVert_2$. If a learned
bilinear factorization is approximately symmetric positive semidefinite on the data,
$K(s,g)=\ip{u(s)}{u(g)}$ for a shared embedding $u$, and polarization with $K(s,s)=1$
gives
\begin{equation}
\lVert u(s)-u(g)\rVert_2^2 \;=\; 2\big(1-\gamma^{d^*(s,g)}\big)
\;=\; 2(1-\gamma)\big({-}V^*(s,g)\big) :
\label{eq:polarization}
\end{equation}
a symmetric PSD bilinear GCIQL solution defines exactly a VIP-style metric embedding
(squared distance $\propto$ negated value). Schoenberg's theorem gives the converse
condition: such a solution exists for every $\gamma$ iff $d^*$ is of negative type. The
antisymmetric part of the learned $K$ measures the deviation --- our probe P4.

\paragraph{QRL.} QRL learns $d_\theta\approx d^*$ directly with an architecture (IQE)
that enforces the quasimetric axioms. Prop.~\ref{prop:qm} says bilinear GCIQL targets a
bounded transform of the same quantity without the constraints; a low-rank approximation
need not preserve Cor.~\ref{cor:mult}, so triangle-inequality violations of
$\hat d:=\log_\gamma\hat K$ certify departure from \emph{any} optimal goal-reaching value
--- our probe P3, requiring no ground truth.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Method & Target & Link / parametrization & Policy, constraints \\
\midrule
Bilinear GCIQL & $K^*=\gamma^{d^*}$ & affine, rank $d$ & in-sample optimal; unconstrained \\
CRL & $\log K^{\pi}+c(g)$ & log, rank $d$ & behavior; unconstrained \\
VIP & $-\phi_\gamma(d^*)$ (KL-reg.) & $-\lVert\phi(s)-\phi(g)\rVert$ & regularized optimal; symmetric \\
QRL & $d^*$ & direct distance & optimal; quasimetric by architecture \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

This table is what motivated the experiment: if all four factorize the same kernel, the
successful one should look like the others. It does not.

\section{Experiments II: the critic as a pure action selector}\label{sec:selector}

The probes say the bilinear critic's advantage is not in its distance. To find where it
is, we remove actor training from the loop entirely. Freeze one proposal policy $\pi_0$
--- the flow-matching BC policy, the same one that scores $9\%$ on cube --- and at every
environment step draw $N$ actions from it, score all $N$ with a trained critic's
$Q(s,a_i,g)$, and execute the argmax. No gradient ever reaches a policy, so nothing about
actor optimization can confound the result. $N{=}1$ executes the raw proposal and must
reproduce the BC floor; success as a function of $N$ reads out exactly the quality of the
critic's action \emph{ordering} on the proposal's support. An earlier one-off version of
this design on the pre-refactor code stack (bridge ``Exp 1'', $N{=}32$) found bilinear
$0.96$ vs.\ monolithic $0.59$; the runs below are the pre-registered re-run: three critic
seeds, both benchmarks, an $N$ dose--response, a CRL arm, and two candidate sources per
benchmark (the BC flows of two different runs) to check that curves are properties of the
critic, not the proposal.

\midrule
cube & bilinear & $0.13$ & $\mathbf{0.92\pm0.02}$ & $0.85\pm0.03$ & $0.93$ \\
cube & GCIQL (MLP) & $0.11$ & $0.61\pm0.09$ & $0.74\pm0.09$ & $0.73$ \\
cube & CRL & $0.14$ & $0.11\pm0.06$ & $0.15\pm0.04$ & $0.28$--$0.34$ \\

\texttt{mw-push} (hard data) & GCIQL & $0.85$ & $0.37\pm0.03$ & $0.20\pm0.05$ & $0.72$ \\

Four findings.

\textbf{(1) On cube, ordering alone reproduces the headline result.} Selecting the best of
eight BC proposals under the bilinear critic scores $0.92$ --- the trained actor's $0.93$
--- with no actor training. The monolithic critic reaches only $0.61$ at the same budget
and needs $N{=}64$ to attain its own trained level ($0.74$ vs.\ $0.73$). The bilinear
advantage is \emph{per-sample ranking efficiency}: its top-of-8 is as good as its
top-of-64. (The pre-registered ``gap widens with $N$'' clause failed, informatively: the
gap is largest at moderate $N$ and the monolithic critic closes most of it given 8$\times$
more samples.)

\textbf{(2) The bilinear form alone contributes nothing.} CRL shares the architecture, the
LayerNorm norm concentration, everything --- and its ranking is at chance at every $N$,
on both candidate sources. What is ranked (an in-sample-optimal value, not
behavior-policy occupancy) is as essential as the form doing the ranking.

\textbf{(3) The ordering advantage replicates on Metaworld --- inside a channel with a
negative sign.} On hard-noise Metaworld data the goal fed to the critic at evaluation is a
fixed dataset frame whose embedded target contradicts each episode's true target
(\S\ref{sec:transfer}); ranking against it is poisoned, and the harder it is trusted the
worse the outcome --- success falls monotonically in $N$ for every critic. But the bilinear
critic degrades at roughly half the monolithic rate, and is above it in \emph{every}
matched (task, $N$, seed, source) comparison: 24 of 24 pairs across both benchmarks. It
also explains an apparent paradox from the training campaign: the trained actor
\emph{gains} $+0.27$ from the same poisoned critic because its BC anchor rations trust,
while argmax selection maximally exploits it.

\textbf{(4) Repairing the goal channel recovers most of the damage --- and flips the
$N{=}8$ sign for the bilinear critic only.} Re-running the Metaworld rows with a
per-episode synthesized goal --- the episode's own observation with the hand/object slots
set to its true target, so the conditioning is context-consistent --- tests the channel
explanation directly (pre-registered: curves flip from monotone-decreasing toward
cube-shaped; the ordering gap persists):

\midrule
\texttt{mw-push}, synthesized goal & bilinear & $0.80$ & $0.83\pm0.15$ & $0.65\pm0.13$ \\
\texttt{mw-push}, synthesized goal & GCIQL & $0.80$ & $0.80\pm0.15$ & $0.40\pm0.05$ \\

The repair lifts every selection cell by $+0.12$ to $+0.43$ (average $+0.23$) over the
fixed-frame table --- direct causal confirmation that the fixed goal frame was poisoning
the ranking. The sign at $N{=}8$ flips to neutral-or-positive for the bilinear critic on
both tasks ($+0.03$/$+0.08$ over the floor, with one seed reaching $20/20$ on push) while
GCIQL stays at-or-below its floor ($0.00$/$-0.02$). The flip is partial: at $N{=}64$
selection still hurts everyone, so an eval-side repair does not fully clean the channel
--- the critics were \emph{trained} with 30\% cross-context relabeled pairs and a
synthesized frame is still slightly off the goal manifold, leaving residual value error
for aggressive argmax to exploit. The remaining lever is the train-side repair (mask the
context dims from $\psi$; set $p_{\mathrm{rand}}{=}0$), which is the one arm of the
program not yet run. One caveat cuts the other way: at pick-place $N{=}64$ GCIQL edges
bilinear ($0.32$ vs.\ $0.27$, within seed noise) --- the only inversion among the 24
fixed-frame and 12 synthesized matched comparisons.

\section{What the bilinear form is leveraging}\label{sec:mechanism}

The ablation program (full log in \texttt{phenomenon.tex}) eliminated the seductive
explanations one by one on cube:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Not boundedness.} A monolithic critic squashed to the bilinear critic's range
($\kappa\tanh(\cdot/\kappa)$, $\kappa{=}75$) recovers $\le3$ of the 19.5 points.
\item \emph{Not norm concentration.} Removing the $\psi$-tower LayerNorm (norm CV
$\le0.09\to$ unconstrained) leaves success unchanged ($0.93$ both).
\item \emph{Not a better metric.} \S\ref{sec:experiments}.
\end{itemize}

What survives has two mutually consistent signatures. First, \emph{off-distribution
coherence}: scoring each critic's value along the \emph{other} policy's eval rollouts, the
monolithic value is coherent only on states its own policy visits (monotonicity
$\rho=0.88$ there, $0.36$ on the bilinear policy's states) while the bilinear value stays
coherent on both ($0.98/0.87$) --- an interaction, not a main effect. Second,
\emph{spectral grounding}: a no-learning graph estimate of the environment's own kernel
(shortest paths over validation data) gives a centered value matrix with an effective rank
of $5$--$11$, top components aligned with the block's three spatial coordinates; the
learned value matrix $\Phi\Psi^\top/\sqrt d$ expresses the \emph{same} $\approx$10-dim
spectrum at $d{=}50$ and $d{=}512$, and policy success saturates exactly where capacity
crosses it ($0.06/0.62/0.84/0.92/0.90/0.93$ at $d=5/10/15/20/30/50$). The picture is
low-rank-MDP/successor-feature structure: $\{V^*(\cdot,g)\}_g$ spans a $\approx$10-dim
function space because $d^*$ factors through the block's 3-D latent; the bilinear head is
a spectral estimator of that family, and a spectrum is a \emph{global} object --- estimated
anywhere, valid everywhere --- which is why its action ordering stays trustworthy on
states beyond the fitting region, and why its top-of-8 already contains the right action
(\S\ref{sec:selector}). The monolithic critic fits values pointwise; its ordering decays
with distance from its own state distribution.

\section{Why it does not transfer, and when it should}\label{sec:transfer}

The cross-environment failures (RQ1) decompose into three confirmed, independent causes;
none of them is ``the bilinear form stops working.''

\textbf{Headroom censoring.} The benchmark measures $S_1-S_0=H\rho$ (BC-relative gain =
headroom $\times$ guidance quality), and on Metaworld expert or mild-noise data BC is
already at $0.89$--$1.00$: any critic is invisible. Opening headroom with hard-noise
collection ($H\to0.3$--$0.5$) made the cube ordering reappear on \texttt{mw-push}
(bilinear $+0.27$ from guidance vs.\ monolithic $+0.05$). The full baseline square on the
hard datasets sharpens this: on \texttt{mw-push} hard-noise, bilinear's $0.87\pm0.08$ is
the best cell outright --- above CRL ($0.80$), monolithic ($0.72$), and FB ($0.55$).
Notably, \emph{FB collapses on hard data} (guidance $\le+0.05$ everywhere; its dominance
of the original campaign was a mild-data phenomenon), while on the goal-diverse
hard-relocation dataset CRL's guidance turns strongly positive on \texttt{mw-pick-place}
($+0.25$, to $0.73\pm0.03$, the best cell there, ahead of bilinear's $+0.12$) ---
consistent with its theory: on diverse play-like data the behavior-policy occupancy CRL
ranks by approaches reachability, whereas on single-corridor data it is uninformative
(chance-level ordering on cube, \S\ref{sec:selector}). No single critic dominates all
regimes; what is stable is that the bilinear critic is never the one that collapses.

\textbf{The goal-observable channel.} Metaworld and ManiSkill observations embed the
episode's immutable target coordinates; OGBench cube observations contain no goal. This
has three consequences: cross-episode relabeled goals are physically unreachable (the
kernel factorizes as $K^*=\mathbf 1[c{=}c']\gamma^{d^*_c}$ over contexts $c$); the
evaluation goal frame is context-inconsistent with almost every episode; and any
target-directed data collection lets BC read the goal from its own observation and
shortcut the guidance channel entirely (a goal-diverse ``play'' collection self-censored
at BC $=0.95$ this way). The selector experiment measures the resulting sign directly
(\S\ref{sec:selector}); FB-style methods are immune because their task conditioning is
inferred from rewards, never from a goal frame.

\textbf{Kernel degeneracy.} On 25-step ManiSkill episodes at $\gamma=0.99$, $K^*$ spans
$[0.78,1]$ --- an affine, nearly rank-1 sliver of its range --- and 48--75\% of
(noisy-)expert steps dwell at the goal, collapsing the relabeled goal marginal. There is
nothing for a low-rank factorization to be better \emph{at}.

The mechanism of \S\ref{sec:mechanism} then makes the positive cross-environment
prediction: the bilinear advantage should track the dimensionality of the goal-relevant
latent (cube-double roughly doubles it), and near-rank-1 task families --- a single 3-D
target, as in most Metaworld tasks --- are exactly the regime where a monolithic critic's
pointwise fit suffices once the channel is clean.

\section{Caveats}

Equations \eqref{eq:vstar}--\eqref{eq:crl} are exact under deterministic dynamics with
single goal visits; under stochasticity $\gamma^{d^*}$ becomes $\E[\gamma^T]$. The
expectile $\tau=0.9$ approximates the in-sample max. $d^*$ is reachability under
dataset-supported policies. Experimental caveats: three seeds per cell throughout, 20--50
episodes per evaluation point; the Metaworld selector floors at $N{=}1$ sit somewhat above
the campaign's BC means (single proposal seed); VIP's runs collapsed to a rank-1 embedding
and are excluded as a reference; and the synthesized-goal repair (\S\ref{sec:selector},
finding 4) is eval-side only --- the train-side channel repair (context-dim masking,
$p_{\mathrm{rand}}{=}0$) remains the one unexercised arm of the program.
